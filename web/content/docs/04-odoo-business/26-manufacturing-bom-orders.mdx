## Why this matters

Everything up to here has been *moving* value: goods between locations, money between
accounts. Manufacturing is where something is **transformed**, raw components become a
finished product, and the temptation is to think that needs a different mental model.
It does not. It needs a third location.

By the end of this chapter you will have manufactured actual `Brake Pad Set` units from
components you stocked yourself. That is the same product you have sold and bought
throughout this part, and you will watch the arithmetic tie back to the recipe exactly.

## Concepts

### A bill of materials is a recipe with an optional routing

A **bill of materials** (BoM) is a recipe: what a product is made of, and in what
quantities. <Term k="bill of materials (`mrp.bom`)">`mrp.bom`</Term> stores a `product_tmpl_id` (or a specific variant), a `product_qty`
the recipe yields, and `bom_line_ids`, the components and their quantities.

Its `type` is usually `normal` (build it), occasionally `phantom` (a kit: the components
appear directly on whatever order references it, with no separate manufacturing step at
all).

A BoM can also list `operation_ids`: work to be done at a **work center**
(`mrp.workcenter`), which is a station on your shop floor such as Assembly, Drill or
Paint, each with a cycle time.

Operations are **optional**. Plenty of real BoMs, including several in this demo
database, have zero. Sometimes a recipe is just "these parts, combined", with no routing
worth tracking.

### The manufacturing order's lifecycle

A <Term k="manufacturing order (`mrp.production`)">`mrp.production`</Term> has six states, more than the documents you have seen so far, because
factory work has a middle that pure paperwork does not:

| `state` | Label |
|---|---|
| `draft` | Draft |
| `confirmed` | Confirmed |
| `progress` | In Progress |
| `to_close` | To Close |
| `done` | Done |
| `cancel` | Cancelled |

A simple order with no tracked work orders or quality checks often skips straight from
`confirmed` to `done` when you mark it complete, which is exactly what you are about to
see. The middle states exist for shop floors that track work order by work order; this
chapter's recipe does not need them.

<Callout type="info" title="Gotcha, found by testing">
  Every document so far, sales orders, purchase orders, invoices, has had no `name` until
  it was confirmed or posted. A manufacturing order **breaks that pattern**: it gets its
  sequence number, `WH/MO/00007` or similar, the moment you create it, still in `draft`.
  Verified directly: a brand-new draft order already had a real name, not `False`. Do not
  assume every document in Odoo waits to be numbered.
</Callout>

### Manufacturing is two more stock moves, through a third location

Confirming a manufacturing order creates two kinds of move: one raw material move per
component, from Stock into a virtual <Term k="Production (location)">**Production**</Term> location, and one finished-good move
from Production back into Stock. Marking the order done is what actually executes them.

<Mermaid
  label="Manufacturing as two more stock moves: components leave Stock for the virtual Production location, and the finished good leaves Production back into Stock"
  chart={`flowchart LR
    S1["WH/Stock<br/>components"] -- "consumed" --> P["Production<br/>(virtual)"]
    P -- "produced" --> S2["WH/Stock<br/>finished good"]`}
/>

Look at that shape again. It is chapter 24's Vendors-to-Stock move and chapter 27's
debit-and-credit, a third time. Components leave Stock and arrive, negative, at
Production. The finished good leaves Production, negative, and arrives, positive, at
Stock.

Nothing is created from nothing, not even in a factory. Quantity always has a source and
a destination, and Production is simply the location where the transformation is
recorded.

## Gotchas

- **A manufacturing order gets its name at creation, not confirmation.** This is the one
  document type in this whole part that breaks the "no name until posted" pattern
  chapters 27 and 28 established. Do not assume it generalises to every document.
- **Confirming reserves; Produce All executes.** Between the two, the raw material and
  finished-good moves sit at `assigned`, which is a real, inspectable state, not a
  placeholder. If component quantities look unconsumed, check whether the order was ever
  actually marked done.
- **Production is a real location with real quants, briefly.** During the moment of
  transformation, components genuinely sit there positive and the output sits there
  negative. That is not a rounding artifact, it is the same double-entry idea inventory
  uses everywhere else, applied to a virtual point instead of a physical shelf.
- **A BoM with zero operations is normal, not incomplete.** Several demo BoMs have none.
  Only add one if there is real work worth tracking against a work center.
- **Component consumption should scale exactly with the BoM ratio.** If a produced
  quantity does not match `product_qty` times each line's `product_qty`, something was
  reserved or consumed against the wrong ratio, not a rounding quirk to shrug off.
