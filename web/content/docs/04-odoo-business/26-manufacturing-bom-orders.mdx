## Why this matters

Everything up to here has been *moving* value: goods between locations, money between
accounts. Manufacturing is where something is **transformed**, raw components become a
finished product, and the temptation is to think that needs a different mental model.
It does not. It needs a third location.

By the end of this chapter you will have manufactured actual `Brake Pad Set` units, the
same product you have sold and bought throughout this part, from components you stocked
yourself, and you will have watched the arithmetic tie back to the recipe exactly.

## Concepts

### A bill of materials is a recipe with an optional routing

<Term k="bill of materials (`mrp.bom`)">`mrp.bom`</Term> says what a product is made of: a `product_tmpl_id` (or a specific variant), a
`product_qty` the recipe yields, and `bom_line_ids`, the components and their quantities.
Its `type` is usually `normal` (build it), occasionally `phantom` (a kit: the components
appear directly on whatever order references it, with no separate manufacturing step at
all).

A BoM can also list `operation_ids`, work to be done against a `mrp.workcenter`
(Assembly, Drill, Paint, whatever your shop has) with a cycle time. Operations are
**optional**. Plenty of real BoMs, including several in this demo database, have zero:
sometimes a recipe is just "these parts, combined", with no routing worth tracking.

### The manufacturing order's lifecycle

A <Term k="manufacturing order (`mrp.production`)">`mrp.production`</Term> has six states, more than the documents you have seen so far, because
factory work has a middle that pure paperwork does not:

| `state` | Label |
|---|---|
| `draft` | Draft |
| `confirmed` | Confirmed |
| `progress` | In Progress |
| `to_close` | To Close |
| `done` | Done |
| `cancel` | Cancelled |

A simple order with no tracked work orders or quality checks often skips straight from
`confirmed` to `done` when you mark it complete, which is exactly what you are about to
see. The middle states exist for shop floors that track work order by work order; this
chapter's recipe does not need them.

<Callout type="info" title="Gotcha, found by testing">
  Every document so far, sales orders, purchase orders, invoices, has had no `name` until
  it was confirmed or posted. A manufacturing order **breaks that pattern**: it gets its
  sequence number, `WH/MO/00007` or similar, the moment you create it, still in `draft`.
  Verified directly: a brand-new draft order already had a real name, not `False`. Do not
  assume every document in Odoo waits to be numbered.
</Callout>

### Manufacturing is two more stock moves, through a third location

Confirming a manufacturing order creates raw material moves, one per component, from
Stock into a virtual <Term k="Production (location)">**Production**</Term> location, and a finished-good move from Production
back into Stock. Marking the order done is what actually executes them.

<Mermaid
  label="Manufacturing as two more stock moves: components leave Stock for the virtual Production location, and the finished good leaves Production back into Stock"
  chart={`flowchart LR
    S1["WH/Stock<br/>components"] -- "consumed" --> P["Production<br/>(virtual)"]
    P -- "produced" --> S2["WH/Stock<br/>finished good"]`}
/>

Look at that shape again. It is chapter 24's Vendors-to-Stock move and chapter 27's
debit-and-credit, a third time. Components leave Stock and arrive, negative, at
Production; the finished good leaves Production, negative, and arrives, positive, at
Stock. Nothing is created from nothing, not even in a factory: quantity always has a
source and a destination, and Production is simply the location where the transformation
is recorded.

## Hands-on

Continuing in `functional`. This chapter's product is the Brake Pad Set from chapter 21,
now with a real recipe. The first movement builds that recipe and runs an order; the
second reads what running it actually did.

<Steps>

<Step>
### Stock two components

Create two simple products, both tracking inventory: `Friction Material` (cost 8.00) and
`Backing Plate` (cost 5.00). Give yourself 20 of each via **Inventory → Operations →
Physical Inventory → New** (under the *Adjustments* heading), setting the counted quantity
for each and applying it.

![The Physical Inventory count screen, listing every stocked product with its On Hand, Counted and Difference columns; Backing Plate, Brake Pad Set (Front) and Friction Material appear among the rows](/screens/ch26-physical-inventory-count.jpg)

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
product.template     +2
      new   Friction Material        list_price=1.0  type=consu
      new   Backing Plate            list_price=1.0  type=consu
stock.quant          +4
      new   Friction Material        location_id=WH/Stock  quantity=20.0
      new   Friction Material        location_id=Inventory adjustment  quantity=-20.0
      new   Backing Plate            location_id=WH/Stock  quantity=20.0
      new   Backing Plate            location_id=Inventory adjustment  quantity=-20.0
```

Even a manual stock count is double-entry: Odoo invents nothing, it moves the 20 units
from a virtual **Inventory adjustment** location into your warehouse, the same pattern
one more time.

</Step>

<Step>
### Give the brake pads a bill of materials

**Manufacturing → Products → Bills of Materials → New**:

- **Product**: `Brake Pad Set`
- **Quantity**: 1
- Components: `Friction Material` × **0.5**, `Backing Plate` × **1**
- One operation: **Bond friction material**, work center **Assembly 1**

![The Brake Pad Set bill of materials, Components tab: Friction Material at 0.50 and Backing Plate at 1.00, BoM Type set to Manufacture this product](/screens/ch26-brake-pad-set-bom.jpg)

</Step>

<Step>
### Manufacture 4 of them

**Manufacturing → Operations → Manufacturing Orders → New**, product
`Brake Pad Set (Front)`, quantity **4**. Look at the order before you do anything else to it:

```console
$ docker compose exec odoo odoo shell -c /etc/odoo/odoo.conf -d functional --no-http
```

```python
>>> mo = env["mrp.production"].search([], order="id desc", limit=1)
>>> mo.name, mo.state
('WH/MO/00007', 'draft')
```

Named, in draft. Press **Confirm**, then **Mark as Done** (skip the quality/work-order
prompts if you are asked; a basic recipe like this one does not need them).

</Step>

<Step>
### Confirming reserved the components

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
mrp.production       +1
      new   WH/MO/00007              state=confirmed  product_qty=4.0
stock.move           +3
      new   Friction Material        reference=WH/MO/00007  product_uom_qty=2.0  state=assigned
      new   Backing Plate            reference=WH/MO/00007  product_uom_qty=4.0  state=assigned
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    reference=WH/MO/00007  product_uom_qty=4.0  state=assigned
```

Three moves for one order: two raw materials, already scaled to 4 units (0.5 x 4 = 2,
1 x 4 = 4), and one for the finished good. All three are only `assigned`, reserved, not
yet moved.

</Step>

<Step>
### Marking it done executes all three at once

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
mrp.production       ~
      chg   WH/MO/00007              state: confirmed -> done
stock.move           ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    state: assigned -> done
      chg   Backing Plate            state: assigned -> done
      chg   Friction Material        state: assigned -> done
stock.quant          +3
      new   Friction Material        location_id=Production  quantity=2.0
      new   Backing Plate            location_id=Production  quantity=4.0
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    location_id=Production  quantity=-4.0
      chg   Backing Plate            quantity: 20.0 -> 16.0
      chg   Friction Material        quantity: 20.0 -> 18.0
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    quantity: 15.0 -> 19.0
```

![Manufacturing order WH/MO/00007, statusbar at Done, quantity 4.00 / 4.00, Components tab showing Friction Material 2.00 consumed and Backing Plate 4.00 consumed](/screens/ch26-manufacturing-order-done.jpg)

Read the Production quants as one small ledger. Friction Material and Backing Plate
arrive there positive (that is where the components went), and Brake Pad Set arrives
there **negative** (that is where the finished good came from). Meanwhile Stock lost 2
Friction, lost 4 Backing Plate, and gained 4 Brake Pad Set. Every number ties exactly to
the recipe: 0.5 and 1.0 per unit, times 4 produced.

```python
>>> friction = env["product.product"].search([("name", "=", "Friction Material")])
>>> plate = env["product.product"].search([("name", "=", "Backing Plate")])
>>> front = env["product.product"].search([("product_tmpl_id.name", "=", "Brake Pad Set")], limit=1)
>>> friction.qty_available, plate.qty_available, front.qty_available
(18.0, 16.0, 19.0)
```

Two components in, one product out, every quantity accounted for.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Gotchas

- **A manufacturing order gets its name at creation, not confirmation.** This is the one
  document type in this whole part that breaks the "no name until posted" pattern
  chapters 27 and 28 established. Do not assume it generalises to every document.
- **Confirming reserves; Mark as Done executes.** Between the two, the raw material and
  finished-good moves sit at `assigned`, which is a real, inspectable state, not a
  placeholder. If component quantities look unconsumed, check whether the order was ever
  actually marked done.
- **Production is a real location with real quants, briefly.** During the moment of
  transformation, components genuinely sit there positive and the output sits there
  negative. That is not a rounding artifact, it is the same double-entry idea inventory
  uses everywhere else, applied to a virtual point instead of a physical shelf.
- **A BoM with zero operations is normal, not incomplete.** Several demo BoMs have none.
  Only add one if there is real work worth tracking against a work center.
- **Component consumption should scale exactly with the BoM ratio.** If a produced
  quantity does not match `product_qty` times each line's `product_qty`, something was
  reserved or consumed against the wrong ratio, not a rounding quirk to shrug off.
