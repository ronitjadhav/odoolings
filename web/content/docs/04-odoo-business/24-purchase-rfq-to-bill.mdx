## Why this matters

Purchase is the mirror of Sales, and it costs much less to learn than the first one did,
because the shapes repeat: a draft document, a confirmation that spawns a transfer, a
bill, a state machine. If you understood chapter 22 you already understand most of this.
(**Procure-to-pay** is just the trade name for that whole run, from asking a supplier for
a price to paying their bill.)

What is genuinely new is the <Term k="three-way match">**three-way match**</Term>, the discipline of tracking what you
ordered, what actually arrived, and what you were billed as three separate numbers.
Reconciling those is one of the oldest problems in business software and one of the most
common sources of real tickets: a supplier bills for ten and ships nine, and something has
to notice.

The other reason to do this chapter properly is that it is where the apps stop looking
like separate apps. You are about to receive the exact part you sold in chapter 22, and
the customer delivery that has been sitting there unable to reserve stock will quietly
change state while you are looking at something else.

## Concepts

### The RFQ lifecycle

`purchase.order` has five states, and the vocabulary shift matters: the same record is
called a **request for quotation** while it is a draft and a **purchase order** once
confirmed.

| `state` | Label |
|---|---|
| `draft` | RFQ |
| `sent` | RFQ Sent |
| `to approve` | To Approve |
| `purchase` | Purchase Order |
| `cancel` | Cancelled |

`to approve` exists because purchases often need authorisation above a threshold, which is
configuration rather than code.

### The three-way match, as three fields

This is the part worth slowing down for. Odoo does not store "is this order done". It
stores three quantities per line and two summary fields on the order:

| Where | Field | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| line | `product_qty` | how much did we order? |
| line | `qty_received` | how much actually arrived? |
| line | `qty_invoiced` | how much were we billed for? |
| order | `receipt_status` | `pending`, `partial` or `full` |
| order | `invoice_status` | `no`, `to invoice` or `invoiced` |

Keeping them apart is the whole point. A supplier who ships nine and bills for ten
produces `product_qty` 10, `qty_received` 9, `qty_invoiced` 10, and that visible
disagreement is what an accounts-payable clerk is paid to chase. A system with one "done"
flag could not express it.

### A stock move always has two ends

Accounting has a rule you will meet properly in chapter 27: value never appears or
vanishes, it moves, and an entry records both ends of the movement. Inventory works
identically. A `stock.move` has a source location and a destination, so receiving goods
does not create them out of nothing. It moves them **from the Vendors location into your
warehouse**, leaving a negative quantity behind.

Two words to have ready before you meet them in the data:

- A **quant** (`stock.quant`) is one row recording how much of a product sits in one
  location. "Quant" is just the name of that row.
- **Vendors** is not a shelf. It is a virtual location standing for the outside world on
  the supplier's side, which is why it is perfectly happy to hold a negative quantity.

That sounds like bookkeeping pedantry until you see it in the data, which you are about
to. It is also why Odoo can tell you what you bought from whom without a separate
purchase history: the negative quants *are* the history.

### The vendor bill is the invoice, mirrored

A vendor bill is a record of `account.move`, the single model Odoo uses for every
accounting document. A field on it, `move_type`, says which kind you are looking at:
`out_invoice` is a customer invoice, `in_invoice` is a vendor bill.

Chapters 27 and 28 teach the accounting itself, so for now just take the shape. Every
accounting entry is written as lines that balance, one side called **debit** and the
other **credit**.

A customer invoice records what the customer owes you (a debit on Account Receivable)
against the sale (a credit on an income account). A vendor bill mirrors that: a debit on
an **expense** account for the cost, and a credit on **Account Payable** for what you now
owe the supplier.

Bills are also numbered out of a different book of entries (a **journal**), which is why
they read `BILL/...` rather than `INV/...`.

### Confirm it

Press **Confirm Order**. Note the title changes from Request for Quotation to Purchase
Order, and a **Receipt** smart button appears.

![The confirmed purchase order P00013: a Receipt smart button reading 1 and a Vendor Bills smart button reading 1, top right, with the statusbar past RFQ and RFQ Sent, at Purchase Order](/screens/ch24-purchase-order-receipt.jpg)

</Step>

<Step>
### Receive the goods

Back on the purchase order:

1. Click the **Receipt** smart button at the top right. It opens the incoming transfer,
   the one named `WH/IN/...`.
2. On the `Brake Pad Set (Front)` line, find the **Quantity** column, the one right next
   to **Demand**, and set it to **10** if it is not already.
3. Press **Validate**. The transfer's status moves to Done.

Demand is what the order asked for and Quantity is what you are saying actually turned up.
They are two columns rather than one for exactly the reason this chapter is about.

</Step>

<Step>
### Bill it

The purchase order's own **Upload Bill** button wants a real file (a scanned invoice) to
attach before it will create anything, so that is not today's path. Instead, go to
**Purchase → Orders → Purchase Orders** (the confirmed-orders list, a different menu item
from Requests for Quotation), tick the checkbox next to your order, and press **Create
Bills** in the toolbar that appears. Yours will not be numbered `P00013`, because the
sequence keeps counting: read every number in this chapter's output, `P00013`,
`WH/IN/00007`, `BILL/2026/08/0002`, as "the record I just made". Odoo drops you straight onto a draft bill, pre-filled from the
PO's lines.

![A draft vendor bill created by the Create Bills bulk action, with the Bill Date field empty and outlined in red as required, the Brake Pad Set line pre-filled at qty 10 and $420.00 total](/screens/ch24-draft-vendor-bill.jpg)

Give it a **Bill Date**, then press **Confirm** to post it.

</Step>

<Step>
### The RFQ, and what it does not have yet

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional

      new   P00013                   state=draft  amount_total=420.0
```

Now open a shell on the same database and pick up the order you just created, which is the
newest `purchase.order` there is:

```console
$ docker compose exec odoo odoo shell -c /etc/odoo/odoo.conf -d functional --no-http
```

```python
>>> po = env["purchase.order"].search([], order="id desc", limit=1)
>>> po.state, po.receipt_status, po.invoice_status
('draft', False, 'no')
```

`receipt_status` is `False`, not `'pending'`. A draft RFQ has no receipt expectation at
all, because nothing has been promised yet.

</Step>

<Step>
### Confirming spawns a receipt

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   state: draft -> purchase
      chg   P00013                   receipt_status: None -> pending
stock.move          +1
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    reference=WH/IN/00007  product_uom_qty=10.0  state=assigned
stock.picking       +1
      new   WH/IN/00007              state=assigned  picking_type_id=YourCompany: Receipts
```

Compare that with chapter 22's confirmation, which produced `WH/OUT` and a Delivery Order.
Same mechanism, opposite direction: `purchase_stock` overrides confirmation the way
`sale_stock` does, each bridge module contributing the transfer its own direction needs.

</Step>

<Step>
### The receipt, and the surprise

This is the diff worth reading twice.

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   receipt_status: pending -> full
      chg   P00013                   invoice_status: no -> to invoice
purchase.order.line ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    qty_received: 0.0 -> 10.0
stock.move          ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    state: assigned -> done
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    state: confirmed -> assigned
stock.picking       ~
      chg   WH/IN/00007              state: assigned -> done
      chg   WH/OUT/00044             state: confirmed -> assigned
stock.quant         +2
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    location_id=Vendors  quantity=-10.0
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    location_id=WH/Stock  quantity=10.0
```

Three separate things happened, and only one of them was your purchase order. Both
`stock.move` rows print the same product name, so read them against the pickings below
them: `assigned -> done` is the receipt's own move, and `confirmed -> assigned` belongs to
a document you never opened.

**The two quants are the move's two ends.** `WH/Stock` gained 10 and `Vendors` lost 10.
Nothing was created; ten brake pads moved across a boundary, and Odoo recorded both sides,
exactly as an accounting entry records a debit and a credit.

**`WH/OUT/00044` changed state, and you did not touch it.** That is the *customer delivery
from chapter 22*, stuck at `confirmed` because there was no stock to reserve. Your receipt
gave it stock, so it advanced to `assigned`: reserved and ready to pick.

Nobody ran anything. It follows from inventory being a shared pool rather than a per-order
ledger, and it is the moment Parts 4 and 5 stop being a tour of separate apps.

**And `invoice_status` moved to `to invoice`.** Receiving is what makes the order billable,
which mirrors chapter 22's delivered-versus-ordered invoicing policy from the buying side.

</Step>

<Step>
### The bill, mirrored

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   BILL/2026/08/0002        move_type=in_invoice  state=posted  amount_total=420.0  payment_state=not_paid
account.move.line   +2
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    account_id=Expenses  debit=420.0
      new   Account Payable          credit=420.0
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   invoice_status: to invoice -> invoiced
purchase.order.line ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    qty_invoiced: 0.0 -> 10.0
```

Put that beside chapter 28's customer invoice and the symmetry is complete:

| | debit | credit |
|---|---|---|
| customer invoice (`out_invoice`) | Account Receivable | income |
| vendor bill (`in_invoice`) | expense | Account Payable |

Same model, same balance rule, opposite direction. And note the number: `BILL/2026/08/0002`
comes from the Purchases journal, not the Sales one.

</Step>

<Step>
### The match, closed

If the shell from step 5 is still open, quit it with `Ctrl-D` and start a new one first.
An Odoo shell holds one database transaction open, so it keeps returning the values it
first read, and none of your browser work since would show up:

```console
$ docker compose exec odoo odoo shell -c /etc/odoo/odoo.conf -d functional --no-http
```

```python
>>> po = env["purchase.order"].search([], order="id desc", limit=1)
>>> l = po.order_line[0]
>>> l.product_qty, l.qty_received, l.qty_invoiced
(10.0, 10.0, 10.0)
>>> po.receipt_status, po.invoice_status
('full', 'invoiced')
```

Ordered, received and billed all agree. That is what a clean purchase looks like, and now
you know which three numbers to compare when one does not.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Gotchas

- **`receipt_status` is `False` on a draft RFQ, not `'pending'`.** Code that assumes it
  always holds one of the three documented values breaks on drafts, and `False` is not in
  the selection.
- **Receiving goods can change documents you were not looking at.** Any outgoing transfer
  waiting on stock may reserve as a side effect. This is correct behaviour and it makes
  "who changed this delivery" a confusing question: often nobody did, directly.
- **A vendor bill needs a Bill Date before it will post.** Odoo does not guess it from
  today, because the bill's date drives the accounting period. Forgetting it is the most
  common reason a `Create Bills` flow stalls.
- **The purchase order form's own button is `Upload Bill`, and it wants a file.** It is a
  file-upload component, not a bill-creation shortcut: without a real attachment it has
  nothing to call. The no-file path is the `Create Bills` bulk action, and it only appears
  on the **Purchase Orders** list view's selection toolbar, not on Requests for Quotation.
- **Ordered, received and billed are three numbers, and they are allowed to disagree.**
  Do not "fix" a mismatch in code without asking which of the three is wrong. That
  disagreement is a feature: it is how an over-billing supplier gets caught.
- **The negative quant at Vendors is not a bug.** New readers try to clean it up. It is
  the other end of the move, and deleting it would destroy the audit trail of what you
  bought.
