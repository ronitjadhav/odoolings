## Why this matters

Purchase is the mirror of Sales, and learning it costs you much less than the first one
did, because the shapes repeat: a draft document, a confirmation that spawns a transfer, a
bill, a state machine. If you understood chapter 22 you already understand most of this.

What is genuinely new is the <Term k="three-way match">**three-way match**</Term>, the discipline of tracking what you
ordered, what actually arrived, and what you were billed as three separate numbers.
Reconciling those is one of the oldest problems in business software and one of the most
common sources of real tickets: a supplier bills for ten and ships nine, and something has
to notice.

The other reason to do this chapter properly is that it is where the apps stop looking
like separate apps. You are about to receive the exact part you sold in chapter 22, and
the customer delivery that has been sitting there unable to reserve stock will quietly
change state while you are looking at something else.

## Concepts

### The RFQ lifecycle

`purchase.order` has five states, and the vocabulary shift matters: the same record is
called a **request for quotation** while it is a draft and a **purchase order** once
confirmed.

| `state` | Label |
|---|---|
| `draft` | RFQ |
| `sent` | RFQ Sent |
| `to approve` | To Approve |
| `purchase` | Purchase Order |
| `cancel` | Cancelled |

`to approve` exists because purchases often need authorisation above a threshold, which is
configuration rather than code.

### The three-way match, as three fields

This is the part worth slowing down for. Odoo does not store "is this order done". It
stores three quantities per line and two summary fields on the order:

| Where | Field | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| line | `product_qty` | how much did we order? |
| line | `qty_received` | how much actually arrived? |
| line | `qty_invoiced` | how much were we billed for? |
| order | `receipt_status` | `pending`, `partial` or `full` |
| order | `invoice_status` | `no`, `to invoice` or `invoiced` |

Keeping them apart is the whole point. A supplier who ships nine and bills for ten
produces `product_qty` 10, `qty_received` 9, `qty_invoiced` 10, and that visible
disagreement is what an accounts-payable clerk is paid to chase. A system with one "done"
flag could not express it.

### A stock move always has two ends

Chapter 27 said value never appears or vanishes, it moves, and that an accounting entry
records both ends. Inventory works identically: a `stock.move` has a source location and a
destination, and receiving goods does not create them out of nothing. It moves them **from
the Vendors location into your warehouse**, leaving a negative quantity behind.

That sounds like bookkeeping pedantry until you see it in the data, which you are about to.
It is also why Odoo can tell you what you bought from whom without a separate purchase
history: the negative quants *are* the history.

### The vendor bill is the invoice, mirrored

A vendor bill is an `account.move` with `move_type = 'in_invoice'`. Same model as chapter
28's customer invoice, opposite direction, mirrored accounts: where a customer invoice
debits Account Receivable and credits income, a vendor bill **debits an expense** and
**credits Account Payable**. It also comes from a different journal, so it is numbered
`BILL/...` rather than `INV/...`.

## Hands-on

Continuing in `functional`.

### Movement 1: run the flow

#### 1. Raise an RFQ

Snapshot first, then **Purchase → Orders → Requests for Quotation → New**:

1. **Vendor**: any partner with a vendor rank (the demo has a couple).
2. Add a line for `Brake Pad Set (Front)`, quantity **10**, unit price **42.00**.
3. Save, and leave it as an RFQ.

#### 2. Confirm it

Press **Confirm Order**. Note the title changes from Request for Quotation to Purchase
Order, and a **Receipt** smart button appears.

![The confirmed purchase order P00013: a Receipt smart button reading 1 and a Vendor Bills smart button reading 1, top right, with the statusbar past RFQ and RFQ Sent, at Purchase Order](/screens/ch24-purchase-order-receipt.jpg)

#### 3. Receive the goods

Open the receipt, set the received quantity to 10, and **Validate**.

#### 4. Bill it

Back on the purchase order, press **Create Bill**. Give the draft bill an
**invoice date**, then post it.

### Movement 2: read what it did

#### 5. The RFQ, and what it does not have yet

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
purchase.order      +1
      new   P00013                   state=draft  amount_total=420.0
```

```python
>>> po.state, po.receipt_status, po.invoice_status
('draft', False, 'no')
```

`receipt_status` is `False`, not `'pending'`. A draft RFQ has no receipt expectation at
all, because nothing has been promised yet.

#### 6. Confirming spawns a receipt

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   state: draft -> purchase
      chg   P00013                   receipt_status: None -> pending
stock.move          +1
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    reference=WH/IN/00007  product_uom_qty=10.0  state=assigned
stock.picking       +1
      new   WH/IN/00007              state=assigned  picking_type_id=YourCompany: Receipts
```

Compare that with chapter 22's confirmation, which produced `WH/OUT` and a Delivery Order.
Same mechanism, opposite direction: `purchase_stock` overrides confirmation the way
`sale_stock` does, and each bridge module contributes the transfer its own direction needs.

#### 7. The receipt, and the surprise

This is the diff worth reading twice.

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   receipt_status: pending -> full
      chg   P00013                   invoice_status: no -> to invoice
purchase.order.line ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    qty_received: 0.0 -> 10.0
stock.move          ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    state: assigned -> done
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    state: confirmed -> assigned
stock.picking       ~
      chg   WH/IN/00007              state: assigned -> done
      chg   WH/OUT/00044             state: confirmed -> assigned
stock.quant         +2
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    location_id=Vendors  quantity=-10.0
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    location_id=WH/Stock  quantity=10.0
```

Three separate things happened, and only one of them was your purchase order.

**The two quants are the move's two ends.** `WH/Stock` gained 10 and `Vendors` lost 10.
Nothing was created; ten brake pads moved across a boundary, and Odoo recorded both sides,
exactly as an accounting entry records a debit and a credit.

**`WH/OUT/00044` changed state, and you did not touch it.** That is the *customer delivery
from chapter 22*, which had been sitting at `confirmed` because there was no stock to
reserve. Your receipt gave it stock, so it advanced to `assigned`, meaning reserved and
ready to pick. Nobody ran anything: it is a consequence of inventory being a shared pool
rather than a per-order ledger. This is the moment Parts 4 and 5 stop being a tour of
separate apps.

**And `invoice_status` moved to `to invoice`.** Receiving is what makes the order billable,
which mirrors chapter 22's delivered-versus-ordered invoicing policy from the buying side.

#### 8. The bill, mirrored

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   BILL/2026/08/0002        move_type=in_invoice  state=posted  amount_total=420.0  payment_state=not_paid
account.move.line   +2
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    account_id=Expenses  debit=420.0
      new   Account Payable          credit=420.0
purchase.order      ~
      chg   P00013                   invoice_status: to invoice -> invoiced
purchase.order.line ~
      chg   Brake Pad Set (Front)    qty_invoiced: 0.0 -> 10.0
```

Put that beside chapter 28's customer invoice and the symmetry is complete:

| | debit | credit |
|---|---|---|
| customer invoice (`out_invoice`) | Account Receivable | income |
| vendor bill (`in_invoice`) | expense | Account Payable |

Same model, same balance rule, opposite direction. And note the number: `BILL/2026/08/0002`
comes from the Purchases journal, not the Sales one.

#### 9. The match, closed

```python
>>> l = po.order_line[0]
>>> l.product_qty, l.qty_received, l.qty_invoiced
(10.0, 10.0, 10.0)
>>> po.receipt_status, po.invoice_status
('full', 'invoiced')
```

Ordered, received and billed all agree. That is what a clean purchase looks like, and now
you know which three numbers to compare when one does not.

## Gotchas

- **`receipt_status` is `False` on a draft RFQ, not `'pending'`.** Code that assumes it
  always holds one of the three documented values breaks on drafts, and `False` is not in
  the selection.
- **Receiving goods can change documents you were not looking at.** Any outgoing transfer
  waiting on stock may reserve as a side effect. This is correct behaviour and it makes
  "who changed this delivery" a confusing question: often nobody did, directly.
- **A vendor bill needs an invoice date before it will post.** Odoo does not guess it from
  today, because the bill's date drives the accounting period. Forgetting it is the most
  common reason a `Create Bill` flow stalls.
- **Ordered, received and billed are three numbers, and they are allowed to disagree.**
  Do not "fix" a mismatch in code without asking which of the three is wrong. That
  disagreement is a feature: it is how an over-billing supplier gets caught.
- **The negative quant at Vendors is not a bug.** New readers try to clean it up. It is
  the other end of the move, and deleting it would destroy the audit trail of what you
  bought.
