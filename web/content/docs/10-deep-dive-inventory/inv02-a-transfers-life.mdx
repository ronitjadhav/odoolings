## Why this matters

The last chapter ended on one sentence: quants change in exactly one way, a transfer is
validated. This chapter is that sentence, slowly, with every button pressed at least once.

Real warehouses are not the happy path. The vendor sends 10 of the 15 you ordered. A
customer brings two brake pad sets back. A bottle leaks on the shelf. Someone creates a
delivery and then the customer cancels. Each of those is a transfer in a state you have to
recognise, and each has a button whose effect on the shelf you have to be able to predict.
When a client says "the system says we have none but there are twelve on the shelf", the
answer is almost always a reservation, and reservations are what this chapter is about.

Chapter 22 and chapter 24 of the journey validated deliveries and receipts in passing.
Here you will watch the states change one at a time, in a database where you know every
number, so nothing is magic.

## Concepts

### Three layers: the transfer, its moves, their move lines

A **transfer** (`stock.picking`) is the document: a number like `LFD/OUT/00001`, a
partner, an operation type, a scheduled date, a state. It carries **moves**
(`stock.move`), one per product line: *8 brake pad sets, from LFD/Stock to Customers*.
A move says what should happen. When Odoo reserves stock for it, it writes **move
lines** (`stock.move.line`) underneath: *8 brake pad sets, taken from LFD/Stock*. If the
pads sat on two shelves, the move would have two move lines, one per shelf. Lots,
serial numbers and packages also live on the move line, which is why the move line
exists at all: it is the layer that says *which exact stock*.

On the transfer form you see the first two layers as columns, and the third behind a
button:

| On screen | Field | Plain words |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand** | `product_uom_qty` on the move | what the document asks for |
| **Quantity** | `quantity` on the move | what is reserved now, and after Validate, what actually moved |
| **Details** | the move lines | where exactly it comes from, which lot, which package |
| the **Picked** box (in Details) | `picked` | a checkbox for the warehouse worker; Odoo ticks it for you when you validate |

Keep *Demand* and *Quantity* apart in your head. Demand is a wish. Quantity is a fact,
first about reservation, then about the real movement. Every surprise in this chapter is
the gap between the two.

### The states, in the order you meet them

A transfer has six states, and a move has seven. You never set either by hand: the
transfer's state is computed from its moves, and the moves change state when you press
a button.

| Transfer shows | Stored as | Meaning | Buttons you see |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | `draft` | typed in, not promised to anyone | Mark as Todo, Validate, Cancel |
| **Waiting Another Operation** | `waiting` | a transfer before this one in a chain is not done (routes chapter) | Cancel |
| **Waiting** | `confirmed` | confirmed, but no stock could be reserved | Check Availability, Validate, Cancel |
| **Ready** | `assigned` | stock is reserved, go and pick it | Validate, Print, Cancel |
| **Done** | `done` | the quants have moved; locked | Print, Return |
| **Cancelled** | `cancel` | nothing happened, nothing will | none |

| Move shows | Stored as | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | `draft` | belongs to a Draft transfer |
| **Waiting Another Move** | `waiting` | waits for a move upstream |
| **Waiting** | `confirmed` | nothing reserved yet |
| **Partially Available** | `partially_available` | some of the demand is reserved |
| **Available** | `assigned` | all of the demand is reserved |
| **Done** | `done` | moved |
| **Cancelled** | `cancel` | dropped |

<Mermaid
  label="A transfer goes Draft, then Waiting or Ready depending on whether stock can be reserved, then Done; Cancel is possible until it is done."
  chart={`stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Draft
    Draft --> Waiting: Mark as Todo, nothing free
    Draft --> Ready: Mark as Todo, stock free
    Waiting --> Ready: Check Availability, or stock arrives
    Ready --> Waiting: Unreserve
    Ready --> Done: Validate
    Waiting --> Done: Validate (type the quantities)
    Draft --> Cancelled: Cancel
    Waiting --> Cancelled: Cancel
    Ready --> Cancelled: Cancel`}
/>

How the transfer's state follows the moves depends on one setting on the transfer, the
**Shipping Policy** (`move_type`, on the Additional Info tab). **As soon as possible**
(the default) says the transfer is Ready when *at least one* move has something
reserved. **When all products are ready** says it is Ready only when *every* move is
fully reserved. The same transfer with 12 of 15 pads reserved is Ready under the first
policy and Waiting under the second.

### Reservation is a promise written on the quant

When Odoo **reserves** stock for a move, it does two things: it creates the move lines,
and it adds the amount to `reserved_quantity` on the quant it took them from. The quant's
`quantity` does not change; the pads are still on the shelf. But **Free to Use**
(`free_qty`, the Stock report's second column) is quantity minus reserved, so the next
transfer that comes along sees fewer pads to take.

Three things decide *when* a transfer reserves. They are on the operation type, as
**Reservation Method**:

| Reservation Method | Stored as | When the transfer reserves |
|---|---|---|
| **At Confirmation** (default) | `at_confirm` | the moment you click Mark as Todo |
| **Manually** | `manual` | only when someone clicks Check Availability |
| **Before scheduled date** | `by_date` | automatically, N days before the Scheduled Date (`reservation_days_before`), with a second N for starred, urgent transfers |

Two more rules, and they explain most "but it says Waiting" tickets:

- **A receipt never waits.** Its source is the Vendor location, and Odoo does not
  reserve from vendor or customer locations, there is nothing to promise. A receipt's
  status bar goes Draft, Ready, Done. The Waiting step is simply not there.
- **Stock arriving wakes transfers up.** When a receipt is validated, Odoo looks for
  Waiting or Partially Available moves of the same product at that location and reserves
  for them, oldest first. You will watch a backorder turn Ready without anyone touching
  it. (Developers: `stock.move._trigger_assign`; the system parameter
  `stock.picking_no_auto_reserve` switches it off.)

**Check Availability** reserves now. **Unreserve** (in the gear menu at the top of the
form) gives the promise back: move lines deleted, `reserved_quantity` down, state back to
Waiting. Neither touches `quantity` on the quant.

### Backorders: what happens to the rest

You validate a transfer with 15 demanded and 12 reserved. Odoo asks **Create
Backorder?** with three answers:

- **Create Backorder**: this transfer is done with 12, and a new transfer, a
  **backorder**, is created for the missing 3, Waiting until stock comes. Its form shows
  **Back Order of** pointing at the original (`backorder_id`).
- **No Backorder**: this transfer is done with 12 and the missing 3 are forgotten. The
  demand on the done transfer is rewritten to 12, and the chatter says so.
- **Discard**: nothing happens.

Whether Odoo asks is itself a setting on the operation type, **Create Backorder**:
**Ask** (the default, the wizard), **Always** (no question, backorder every time) or
**Never** (no question, never a backorder). A receipt behaves the same way when the
vendor ships short.

### Returns: a transfer in the mirror

A done transfer has a **Return** button. It opens a wizard listing the transfer's
products, **every quantity at 0**, so you type what actually comes back. **Return**
creates a new transfer in the opposite direction, using the operation type named as
**Returns Type** on the original's operation type: a delivery returns through
Receipts, a receipt returns through Delivery Orders. The new transfer's **Source
Document** reads *Return of LFD/OUT/00001*, and each of its moves remembers the move it
undoes (`origin_returned_move_id`). **Return All** fills every quantity for you.
**Return for Exchange** returns and at the same time creates a fresh outgoing transfer
for replacements. A return is a normal transfer: it is Ready, you validate it, the
quants move. You can even return a return.

### Scrap: stock that leaves without a customer

A **scrap** (`stock.scrap`) takes a quantity of a product out of a location and moves it
to an **Inventory Loss** location. The form has a **Replenish Quantities** box
(`should_replenish`): tick it and Odoo asks the replenishment rules to order the same
quantity again (the replenishment chapter). **Scrap Reason** is a list of tags you
create as you go, so a report can say how much leaked versus how much expired. Scrap
more than there is and Odoo warns you before it lets you.

<Callout type="info" title="On Odoo 18 this differs">
  Odoo 18 ships a dedicated `Virtual Locations/Scrap` location, flagged *Is a Scrap
  Location*. Odoo 19 removed the flag and the location: a scrap's **Scrap Location** is
  any Inventory Loss location (`usage = inventory`), and a fresh database has exactly one,
  `Inventory adjustment`, shared with stock counts. And the grouping field on a transfer's
  Additional Info tab is a `stock.reference` in 19, where 18 had a *Procurement Group*.
</Callout>

### Cancel, and the smaller buttons

**Cancel** asks you to confirm, then sets the transfer and its moves to Cancelled. A
cancelled move keeps its Demand on screen as a record of what was asked, but its
Quantity is 0 and any reservation is released. Rules in the routes chapter decide
whether cancelling one transfer in a chain cancels the next (`propagate_cancel`).

The gear menu on a transfer form holds the rest: **Unreserve**, **Lock/Unlock** (a done
transfer is locked; unlocking lets a manager correct quantities after the fact, which
also rewrites quants, so it is a last resort), **Scrap** (a scrap pre-filled from this
transfer) and **Split** (move some lines to a new transfer). The star next to the
reference is **priority**: a starred transfer sorts first and, with *Before scheduled
date*, reserves earlier.

### Email, text message, signature, printing

Three settings under **Inventory → Configuration → Settings → Shipping** change what
Validate does on a delivery. **Email Confirmation** mails the customer when the delivery
is done. **Text Confirmation** does the same by SMS, and it is **ticked on a fresh
database**; untick it on a project with no SMS credits. **Signature** asks for a
signature on the delivery slip. On the operation type, the **Hardware** tab lists what to
**Print on Validation**: the delivery slip, the return slip, product labels. Each is
off by default, and ticking one shows the label format to use.

## Gotchas

- **Mark as Todo is not Validate.** The first reserves, the second moves. A transfer can
  sit in Ready for a week with the goods still on the shelf, and the Stock report's On
  Hand will not budge; only Free to Use does. "It says we shipped it" usually means
  somebody confirmed and nobody validated.
- **The green badge in Draft is a forecast.** It said Available on a line asking for 15
  pads when 12 were free. Only after Mark as Todo does the badge reflect a real
  reservation. Trust the Quantity column, not the colour.
- **No Backorder rewrites history, politely.** It sets the demand on the done transfer to
  what was shipped and logs the change. The customer's missing three are then nowhere in
  Inventory; only the chatter remembers. On a sales order they still show as not
  delivered, which is the right place to catch them.
- **The return wizard starts at zero.** Click Return then Return without typing a quantity
  and you get a return of nothing. Return All is the shortcut when everything comes back.
- **The previous chapter's last check turns red now.** It asserted 20, 12 and 1 on the
  shelf. That is expected: checks describe a chapter's end state, and `start inv02`
  rebuilds and checks the previous chapter before replaying this one.
