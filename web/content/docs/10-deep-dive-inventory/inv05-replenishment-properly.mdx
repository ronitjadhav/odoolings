## Why this matters

Until now every purchase and every manufacturing order in this track came from a person
or from a sales order. A real depot cannot work like that: someone would have to check
every product every morning. Odoo's answer is the **reordering rule**: "keep at least
this many here, and when you refill, refill up to that many". The rules come with one
screen, **Replenishment**, which lists what needs ordering and why.

It is also the screen that brings integrators the most questions. "Why did Odoo order 42
bottles when we were only 7 short?" "Why did nothing happen overnight?" "What does *No
rule has been found* mean?" Each one has an exact answer, made of four things: the
forecast, the horizon, the lead times and the numbers on the rule. This chapter builds
each of them on the depot, with real numbers, until 42 is obvious.

## Concepts

### A reordering rule

A **reordering rule** (`stock.warehouse.orderpoint`, "orderpoint" in the code) is about
one product in one location, here `LFD/Stock`. The Replenishment screen shows one line
per rule:

| Column | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **Location** | where the stock must be; one rule per product and location |
| **On Hand**, **Forecast** | what is on the shelf now, and what will be there on the forecast date (next section) |
| **Route** | how to refill: Buy, Manufacture, or another route. Left empty, Odoo picks one and shows it in grey |
| **Vendor** | which vendor line of the product to buy from. Left empty, Odoo picks the first that fits, also in grey |
| **Bill of Materials** | which bill of materials to use when the route manufactures |
| **Trigger** | **Auto**: the scheduler orders by itself. **Manual**: the line waits on this screen for you |
| **Min**, **Max** | refill when the forecast drops below Min, and refill up to Max |
| **Multiple** | order whole packs only: one of the product's packagings, or the vendor's unit |
| **To Order** | what Odoo would order right now. On a Manual line you may type over it |
| **Deadline** | the last day to order before the forecast falls below Min |

Route, Vendor, Bill of Materials, Trigger, Multiple and Deadline are optional columns:
switch them on from the toggle at the right end of the header row.

### How Odoo finds the number

Every To Order follows the same four steps:

1. Find the **forecast date**: today, plus the **lead time** of the route, plus the
   **horizon**.
2. Take the **forecast** for that date: on hand, plus everything planned to come in,
   minus everything planned to go out, up to that date. An order already started for this
   rule counts as coming in.
3. If the forecast is **not below Min**, To Order is 0.
4. Otherwise To Order is **Max minus the forecast**, rounded **up** to a whole
   **Multiple**. (If Max is smaller than Min, Min is used as Max.)

<Mermaid
  label="To Order is zero when the forecast is at or above Min; otherwise it is Max minus the forecast, rounded up to the Multiple. With a forecast of minus 7, Max 30 and packs of 6, that is 37 rounded up to 42."
  chart={`flowchart LR
    F["Forecast at<br/>today + lead time + horizon"] --> Q{"below Min?"}
    Q -- "no" --> Z["To Order = 0"]
    Q -- "yes" --> M["Max - forecast<br/>30 - (-7) = 37"]
    M --> R["round up to the Multiple<br/>7 packs of 6 = 42"]`}
/>

That is the whole secret of "why 42": the forecast was -7, Max was 30, so 37 were
missing, and the vendor sells packs of 6, so 7 packs. Notice that the rule does not
order what is *missing below Min*; it orders what fills the shelf *up to Max*.

### Lead time and horizon

The **lead time** is how long a refill takes. Odoo adds it up from the route the rule
would use:

| Part | Where you set it | Counts when |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor Lead Time** | the vendor line, product form, Purchase tab | the route buys |
| **Days to Purchase** | Inventory settings, Advanced Scheduling | the route buys |
| **Manufacturing Lead Time** (`produce_delay`) | the bill of materials | the route manufactures |
| **Days to prepare Manufacturing Order** (`days_to_prepare_mo`) | the bill of materials | the route manufactures |
| **Delay** | a pull rule of the route (`delay`) | the rule is on the way |
| **No Vendor Found**, **No BoM Found** | nobody: Odoo adds 365 days | buying with no usable vendor, manufacturing with no BoM |

The last line is a warning in disguise: a year of lead time pushes the forecast date far
away, so the rule sees every planned move for a year and orders for all of it.

The **horizon** is extra look-ahead on top of the lead time: the company's
**Replenishment Horizon**, 365 days by default. With 365, any confirmed demand in the
next year makes a line appear today. Set it to 0 and Odoo orders "just in time", only for
what falls inside the lead time. The **HORIZON** box in the Replenishment side panel
changes it for that screen only; the scheduler uses the company setting.

Two more dates move the *demand* rather than the rule: the product's **Customer Lead
Time** (Inventory tab, `sale_delay`) sets how far after the order a delivery is planned,
and **Security Lead Time for Sales** (Inventory settings) plans every delivery that many
days earlier. Either can move an outgoing move inside or outside the forecast date.

To see a forecast with your own eyes, use the product's **Forecasted** smart button: the
report adds on hand, incoming and outgoing up to a date, and lists who needs what. To see
the past, use **Inventory at Date** on Reporting → Stock.

### Manual, automatic, and the scheduler

A **Manual** rule only shows up in Replenishment; nothing happens until you click
**Order**. An **Auto** rule is handled by the **scheduler**, the scheduled action
*Procurement: run scheduler*, which runs once a day. For every Auto rule with something
to order it creates the purchase or manufacturing order, the same as clicking Order.
It also retries reservations of waiting moves and tidies quants.

The scheduler cannot show you an error. When a rule fails (no route, no vendor), it logs
an **Exception** activity on the product instead, for the product's **Responsible**
(Inventory tab; by default whoever created the product), or for OdooBot when the field is
empty. Read your activities.

### The Replenishment report

Opening **Inventory → Operations → Replenishment** does more than read: for every
storable product whose forecast is negative and that has no rule, it **creates** a Manual
rule named *Replenishment Report*, with Min and Max at 0, made by OdooBot. Those are the
"suggestions". When you order one and nothing is left to order, Odoo deletes it again. A
rule you make yourself, or one you switch to Auto, stays.

| On a line | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Order** | orders To Order once; the trigger stays Manual |
| **Automate** | switches the trigger to Auto and orders once, now |
| **Snooze** | hides the line until a date: 1 day, 1 week, 1 month or a custom date |
| warning icon after the Route | no way to replenish: no route, vendor or bill of materials fits |
| info icon after the Route | the Replenishment Information window: the lead time, step by step |
| warning icon after the Forecast | ordering To Order would end above Max (packs, or receipts already planned) |

Select several lines and the header offers **Order**, **Order To Max** and **Snooze**.
*Order To Max* orders Max minus the forecast even for lines that are not below Min. The
filters **To Reorder** (To Order above 0) and **Not Snoozed** are on by default.

## Gotchas

- **An Auto rule waits for the scheduler.** Creating it or switching it to Auto orders
  nothing; the scheduler runs once a day. **Automate** is the exception: it switches
  *and* orders now. "Nothing happened overnight" usually means the rule is Manual, or the
  forecast is not below Min.
- **Setting Min and Max on a suggestion, then clicking Order, can lose them.** The report
  made that rule (OdooBot) and it is Manual, so once nothing is left to order Odoo deletes
  it, your Min and Max with it. Use **Automate**, or create the rule yourself from the
  product.
- **No vendor means "No rule has been found".** The Buy route only answers for products
  with a vendor line. Odoo also adds 365 days of lead time when a buy rule finds no usable
  vendor, which makes the forecast date a year later than you think.
- **A one-year horizon orders for next year's demand today.** With the default 365 days,
  any confirmed outgoing move in the next twelve months is in the forecast. Lower the
  Replenishment Horizon if your buyers want just-in-time orders.
- **The Multiple rounds up, past Max.** 37 became 42, and the warning icon after the
  Forecast says so. If overshooting is not acceptable, choose a smaller packaging or none.
- **The scheduler fails silently.** No error dialog, only an Exception activity on the
  product, for its Responsible. Products created by a script or an import often have no
  Responsible, and then the activity goes to OdooBot, which nobody reads.
