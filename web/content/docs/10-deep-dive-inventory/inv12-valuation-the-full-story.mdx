## Why this matters

Chapter 30 met valuation in the `functional` database: two switches on the product
category, a value on every stock move, and an account on the Customers location. This
chapter is the full tour, on stock you know by heart.

It matters because a stock value is a number someone signs. The accountant closes the
month on it, the controller compares it to the shelves, and the margin on every sale is
built on it. In the depot, every product is worth $0 right now: no costs were ever
entered. By the end you will have a standard cost on the oil, a product that lived under
FIFO and then AVCO, a vendor bill that changed a receipt's value, freight added on top, a

## Concepts

### Four places decide a value

Valuation answers two questions, as chapter 30 put it: **what is a unit worth** (the
costing method) and **when do the books hear about it** (the valuation). In Odoo 19 the
answers live in four places, from the most general to the most specific:

| Where | What it holds | Who sees it in Community |
|---|---|---|
| **Company** | `cost_method` (Standard Price by default), `inventory_valuation` (Periodic), `inventory_period` (Manual), the stock journal and the stock valuation account | nobody: no view shows them; shell, or the category |
| **Product category** | **Costing Method** and **Inventory Valuation**, overriding the company; its accounts | the two fields: stock managers. The accounts: only with **Show Full Accounting Features** |
| **Product** | the **Cost** (`standard_price`), and the computed `cost_method` and `valuation` | everyone |
| **Stock move** | its `value`, set when it is done | stock managers, through the reports |

An empty Inventory Valuation on a category means "use the company's". The company
values are also copied as defaults for new categories, which is why the depot's
categories already say Standard Price and Periodic.

Only stock **the company owns** in an **internal or transit** location is valued. The
coolant from chapter 9 belongs to Nordic Lubricants: it has a cost, and it is worth $0 to
you.

### Where the value lives

There is no `stock.valuation.layer` in Odoo 19 (chapter 30). The value of each movement
is stored on the move itself, in `stock.move.value`, once the move is done. A move counts
when it crosses the company's boundary: **in** from a non-valued location (a vendor,
production, an adjustment) into a valued one, or **out** the other way. The internal
steps of a two- or three-step route are worth nothing on their own.

Next to the moves sits a history model, **`product.value`**. Every change of a product's
cost writes a row, and so does a manual correction of a move's value. That history is
what lets Odoo answer "what was this worth on 30 June?" (**Inventory at Date**).

An incoming move gets its value from the first source that can answer, in this order:

| # | Source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a **manual** value (`product.value` on the move) | Actions → Adjust Valuation |
| 2 | the posted **vendor bill** | billed at $9.90 instead of $9 |
| 3 | the **production** cost | a manufacturing order |
| 4 | the **purchase order** line, "not billed" yet | ordered at $9 |
| 5 | the original move, for a **return** | a customer returns a filter |
| 6 | the product's **cost** | an inventory adjustment |

**Landed costs** are added on top, unless a manual value is set: a manual value wins over
everything. So a receipt is worth the PO price when it arrives, and it is re-valued when
the bill is posted. An outgoing move is valued by the costing method.

### How each costing method answers

<Mermaid
  label="The same purchases under three costing methods: Standard values everything at the typed cost, FIFO consumes the oldest receipt first, and AVCO keeps one running average"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  B["Buy 10 at $8<br/>then 10 at $10"] --> S["Standard<br/>every unit at the Cost<br/>you typed"]
  B --> F["FIFO<br/>sell 12: 10 x $8 + 2 x $10<br/>= $100"]
  B --> A["AVCO<br/>average $9<br/>sell 12: 12 x $9 = $108"]`}
/>

- **Standard Price**: every unit, in and out, at the product's Cost. Changing the Cost
  re-values the whole stock at once and leaves a `product.value` row.
- **FIFO**: each receipt keeps its value. A delivery consumes the oldest receipts first.
  What is left of each receipt is its `remaining_qty` and `remaining_value`, and the
  product's Cost follows: total value divided by quantity.
- **AVCO**: one average, recomputed at each receipt: (value before + value received) ÷
  quantity. Deliveries leave at the average.

The costing method has nothing to do with the **removal strategy** of chapter 9. The oil
category takes the newest bottle off the shelf (LIFO removal) and values every bottle at
a standard cost. One decides which unit moves, the other what it is worth.

Switching a category's costing method is not free. Odoo recomputes the products' cost as
if the new method had always applied: a FIFO product switched to AVCO gets the average of
its whole history.

### Two ways to tell the books

**Periodic (at closing)** keeps the books away from the warehouse. Vendor bills go to an
expense account, deliveries post nothing, and at the end of a period one **closing
entry** brings the stock valuation account to the stock's value, against the **stock
variation** account. You trigger it from the **Inventory Valuation** report, which has
no menu in Community: it lives at `/odoo/stock-valuation-closing`. With an Inventory
Period of **Daily** or **Monthly** on the company, the scheduled action "Stock Account:
Inventory Valuation Closing" posts it for you; **Manual**, the default, leaves it to you.

**Perpetual (at invoicing)** writes as the documents happen, and the label is accurate:
the entries ride on the bill and the invoice.

- The vendor bill line goes to the stock valuation account instead of an expense. With
  Standard Price, a bill above the cost sends the difference to a price difference
  account.
- The customer invoice gets **cost of goods sold** lines (`display_type` `cogs`): credit
  stock valuation, debit the expense account, at the product's cost when the invoice is
  posted.
- Receipts and deliveries post nothing, unless a location on one side has a valuation
  account (chapter 30 set one on Customers; Odoo shows the field on inventory-loss and
  production locations).

| Document | Periodic | Perpetual |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor bill | expense account | stock valuation account |
| Delivery | nothing | nothing, unless a location has an account |
| Customer invoice | sales and tax only | plus the cost of goods sold lines |
| Period end | one closing entry | nothing left to close |stock valuation vs variation"]
  end
  subgraph R["Perpetual"]
    R1["bill: stock valuation"] --> R2["delivery: nothing"] --> R3["invoice: COGS lines<br/>stock valuation vs expense"]
  end`}
/>

Both can share one stock valuation account, but the closing compares **all** stock value
with that account's balance. Close the period before a category moves to perpetual, or
the closing will "correct" entries the invoices already made.

### Landed costs

A **landed cost** adds the price of getting goods in (freight, customs, insurance) to the
receipts. The **Landed Costs** setting installs `stock_landed_costs`. A landed cost lists
transfers and cost lines; each line is split over the received products by one method:

| Split method | Share of each product |
|---|---|
| **Equal** | the same for every line |
| **By Quantity** | by units received |
| **By Current Cost** | by the value received |
| **By Weight** / **By Volume** | by the products' weight or volume |

It only works for **FIFO and AVCO** products: a standard cost is, by definition, not
changed by what you paid. Validating it raises the moves' value; it writes a journal
entry only for perpetual products. A service product marked **Is a Landed Cost** can sit
on a vendor bill, whose **Create Landed Costs** button links the two.

### Lot valuation

On a product tracked by lots or serials, **Valuation by Lot/Serial** gives every lot its
own cost. Each receipt must then carry a lot, deliveries leave at the lot's cost, and the
product's value is the sum of its lots. You cannot switch it on while some stock has no
lot. It suits a part whose price varies by batch, such as a serial-numbered diagnostic
tablet bought at different prices.

### Where to look

- **Inventory → Reporting → Stock**: **Unit Cost** and **Total Value** per product, and
  **Inventory at Date** for the past.
- Click a **Total Value**: the **Valuation** list of the valued moves, with their value
  and what remains of each receipt.
- Click a **Unit Cost**: the **Unit Cost History**, every change and the average after
  it. FIFO products have none.
- `/odoo/stock-valuation-closing`: the **Inventory Valuation** report and its **Generate
  Entry** button.

## Gotchas

- **The company switches have no screen.** Cost method, valuation and period on the
  company are only reachable from a shell in Community. Set the category instead, which
  overrides them.
- **Changing the costing method re-values history.** Switching FIFO to AVCO recomputed
  the filters from $80 to $72. Do it at a period end, and close the period right after.
- **Removal strategy is not costing.** LIFO removal on the oil decides which bottle
  leaves the shelf; its value still follows its costing method.
- **Landed costs skip standard-cost products.** Odoo refuses them: a standard cost is a
  number you choose, not one you paid.
- **Close before going perpetual.** The closing compares all stock with the stock
  account. Switch a category to perpetual after a closing, or the next closing undoes
  cost of goods sold that invoices already posted.
- **A manual value wins over everything.** Adjust Valuation on a receipt overrides the
  bill and drops its landed costs. Use it for a real correction, with a description.
