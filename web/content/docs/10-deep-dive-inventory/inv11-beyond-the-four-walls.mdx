customers, vendors shipping on your behalf, and parts sent out to a subcontractor. ·

## Why this matters

So far every product came in through the depot's door and went out through it. Real
businesses leak at the edges. A customer wants to know what shipping costs before they
order, and a parcel needs a tracking number. Some products you sell but never stock: the
tyre wholesaler ships them to your customer directly. And some work you do not do
yourself: you send parts to a workshop that sends back a finished product.

Each of these has a model in Odoo, and each one has a trap. This chapter sets up two
delivery methods and ships an order with a tracking number. Then it dropships tyres from
a vendor, and has an engine rebuilt by a subcontractor with your own oil.

## Concepts

### Delivery methods

A **delivery method** (`delivery.carrier`) is a way to ship that has a price. It is tied
to a **delivery product**: a service product that appears as the shipping line on the
sales order. Community has two providers:

- **Fixed Price**: one price for every order. It is stored as the delivery product's
  sales price.
- **Based on Rules**: a list of price rules, tried in order. The first rule whose
  condition is true sets the price. If none matches, the method is not available for
  that order.

A rule reads: *if* a **variable** compared with a value is true, *then* the price is a
**base price** plus a **price** times a **factor**. Both the variable and the factor are
chosen from the same five:

| Variable | What Odoo adds up over the order lines |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | product weight times quantity |
| **Volume** | product volume times quantity |
| **Weight * Volume** | weight times volume, per line |
| **Price** | the order total without shipping |
| **Quantity** | the number of units |

The operators are `=`, `<=`, `<`, `>=` and `>`. A courier that charges $8 up to 5 kg,
then $5 plus $1 per kg up to 30 kg, is two rules. A 31 kg order matches neither, so the
courier simply does not offer itself.

Around the price sit a few more fields:

- **Margin on Rate** (a percentage) and **Additional margin** (an amount) are added on
  top. They apply to Based on Rules and to the carrier connectors, **never to Fixed
  Price**.
- **Free if order amount is above**: the shipping line drops to 0 when the order, without
  shipping, reaches the amount.
- The **Availability** tab hides the method from orders that do not fit: **Countries**,
  **States**, **Zip Prefixes**, **Max Weight**, **Max Volume**, **Must Have Tags** and
  **Excluded Tags** (product tags).
- **Invoicing Policy**: **Estimated cost** bills what the order said. **Real cost**
  updates the shipping line with the price computed when the delivery is validated,
  useful with a connector that returns the carrier's real charge.
- **Tracking Link**: a URL with `<shipmenttrackingnumber>` in it. Odoo puts the
  transfer's tracking reference in its place and shows a **Tracking** button.
- **Routes**: routes you may pick per order because of this carrier. They are the routes
  marked selectable on shipping methods (`shipping_selectable`).

Return labels and shipping insurance exist as fields, but only the connectors fill them:
Fixed Price and Based on Rules cannot generate a label.

### The carrier on the order and the transfer

**Add shipping** on a quotation opens a small wizard. It computes the weight of the order,
lets you change it, and asks the method for a rate. Confirming it writes the carrier on
the order and adds the shipping line.

When the order is confirmed, the carrier travels down to the transfers. Each rule of a
multi-step delivery has **Propagation of carrier** (`propagate_carrier`), so in a three-step
depot the Pick, the Pack and the Delivery all carry it. On the transfer's **Additional
Info** tab you find:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Carrier** | the delivery method |
| **Tracking Reference** | the carrier's number for the parcel |
| **Weight** | the weight of the products, computed |
| **Weight for shipping** | what you declare: the products plus packaging, editable |

Customs data lives on the product: **HS Code**, the Harmonized System number customs use
to classify goods, and **Origin of Goods**, the country it was made in. Connectors print
them on customs forms; in Community they are information you keep.

### Dropshipping

**Dropshipping** means you sell a product, and the vendor delivers it straight to your
customer. It never sits on your shelves. The module adds a **Dropship** route. Tick it on
a product, and a confirmed sale creates a purchase order instead of a delivery.

<Mermaid
  label="A dropshipped sale: the sale order creates a purchase order to the vendor, and one Dropship transfer moves the goods from Vendors to Customers without touching the depot"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  SO["Sale order<br/>Garage Nord, 4 tyres"] -->|Dropship route| PO["Purchase order<br/>Pneu Direct<br/>Deliver To: Dropship"]
  PO -->|confirm| DS["DS transfer<br/>Vendors → Customers"]
  DS -.->|never| WH[("LibreFleet<br/>Depot")]`}
/>

The purchase order's **Deliver To** is the **Dropship** operation type, and its
**Dropship Address** is your customer. The transfer is a single move from Vendors to
Customers. The operation type belongs to the company, not to a warehouse, and the
transfer's `is_dropship` flag is how code tells it apart.

### Subcontracting

**Subcontracting** is buying a product that a partner builds from your components. The
module adds a bill of materials type, **Subcontracting**, with a list of
**Subcontractors**. When you buy that product from one of them, Odoo knows two things
happen: you send the components out, and you receive the finished product.

<Mermaid
  label="Subcontracting: the purchase order creates a resupply transfer that sends the oil to the Subcontracting location, a hidden production that consumes it there, and a receipt of the rebuilt engine"
  chart={`flowchart LR
  PO["Purchase order<br/>Atelier Moteur<br/>1 Rebuilt Engine"] --> RES["Resupply (RES)<br/>LFD/Stock → Subcontracting<br/>5 oil"]
  PO --> IN["Receipt (IN)<br/>engine arrives"]
  RES --> SBC["Subcontracting production (SBC)<br/>consumes 5 oil<br/>at Subcontracting"]
  SBC -->|finished by validating| IN`}
/>

- **Subcontracting** is one location for the whole company, of type internal. Stock
  there is yours, held by the subcontractor.
- The **Resupply Subcontractor** operation type (prefix RES) carries the components from
  your stock to that location. In Odoo 19 the **Resupply Subcontractor on Order** route
  is a warehouse route, switched on by the warehouse's **Resupply Subcontractors**
  option, not something you tick per product.
- The **Subcontracting** production (prefix SBC) is a manufacturing order you rarely
  open. It is created with the receipt, and validating the receipt finishes it: the
  components are consumed at the Subcontracting location, the product comes in.

### The reception report

**Reception Report**, a setting under Operations, adds an **Allocation** button on
receipts. It lists the receipt's products next to the orders waiting for them, so the
person unloading the truck knows which pallet goes to which order. An operation type can
show it automatically at validation (**Show Reception Report at Validation**).

<Callout type="info" title="In the field">
  Enterprise adds the carrier connectors that price, label and track for real: DHL
  Express, FedEx, UPS, USPS, bpost, Easypost, Sendcloud, Shiprocket, Starshipit and
  Envia.com. They appear in the settings under Shipping Connectors. A connector is a
  delivery method with a different provider, so everything in this chapter still applies.
  The **Dispatch Management System** setting (`stock_fleet`) plans packs into vehicle
  trips; it is named here and not covered.
</Callout>

### A courier priced by weight

<UiOrShell>
<Tab>

**New** again:

- **Delivery Method:** `Courier by Weight`
- **Provider:** Based on Rules
- **Delivery Product:** create `Courier by Weight`
- **Tracking Link:** `https://track.example.com/<shipmenttrackingnumber>`

The form grows a **Pricing** tab. **Add a line**:

![The Create Pricing Rules dialog with Condition Weight, operator less than or equal, 5.00, and Delivery Cost $ 8.00 + $ 0.00 * Weight highlighted](/screens/inv11-price-rule.jpg)

Condition **Weight** `<=` `5`, Delivery Cost `8` plus `0` times Weight. **Save & New**,
then a second rule: Weight `<=` `30`, cost `5` plus `1` times **Weight**. **Save & Close**:

![The Courier by Weight method with Provider Based on Rules highlighted, and its Pricing tab highlighted, listing the rule for weight up to 5.00 at a fixed $ 8.00, and the rule for weight up to 30.00 at $ 5.00 plus $ 1.00 times weight](/screens/inv11-courier-rules.jpg)

The rules are tried from the top. Up to 5 kg the first one wins and costs $8. From just
over 5 kg up to 30, the second one charges $5 plus $1 per kg. Save.

</Tab>
<Tab>

```python
>>> rule = lambda top, base, per: (0, 0, {"variable": "weight", "operator": "<=", "max_value": top, "list_base_price": base, "list_price": per, "variable_factor": "weight"})
>>> courier = Carrier.create({"name": "Courier by Weight", "delivery_type": "base_on_rule", "product_id": fee("Courier by Weight").id, "tracking_url": "https://track.example.com/<shipmenttrackingnumber>", "price_rule_ids": [rule(5, 8, 0), rule(30, 5, 1)]})
>>> courier.price_rule_ids.mapped("name")
['if weight <= 5.00 then fixed price $\xa08.00', 'if weight <= 30.00 then fixed price $\xa05.00 plus $\xa01.00 times weight']
```

`list_base_price` is the fixed part, `list_price` the part multiplied by
`variable_factor`. (`\xa0` is the non-breaking space Odoo puts after the currency sign.)

## Gotchas

- **Set the delivery product before the Fixed Price.** The price is stored on the
  product, so choosing or creating the product overwrites what you typed.
- **Margins do not touch Fixed Price.** Margin on Rate and Additional margin apply to
  Based on Rules and the connectors only.
- **No matching rule, no method.** An order heavier than the last rule allows makes the
  courier say "Not available for current order". End a rule list with a catch-all rule
  if every order must ship.
- **A carton taken whole remembers its pallet.** After a pallet is moved as one package
  (chapter 9), its cartons keep the pallet as their destination container. Taking one
  carton out whole fails with "Can't move a container having packages in another
  location". Clear the Destination Package in Details to move the contents loose.
- **Taking exactly half a package can block the next step.** In the Odoo 19 build this
  tutorial uses, picking 6 of a carton of 12 leaves the following Pack waiting forever:
  Odoo wrongly records that the whole carton moved. Pick any other quantity, or type the
  quantity on the waiting step by hand.
- **A dropship never touches your stock**, so it never shows in your warehouse's
  forecast either. The sale counts as delivered when the vendor's transfer is validated.
