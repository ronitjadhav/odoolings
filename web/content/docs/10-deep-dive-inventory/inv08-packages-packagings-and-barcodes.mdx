· **Time:** ~3 h · **Checkpoint:** none, this chapter writes no module. It continues the
`warehouse` database.

## Why this matters

Until now every bottle of oil moved on its own. A real warehouse does not work like
that. Nordic Lubricants delivers cartons on a pallet, the storekeeper puts the whole
pallet away in one go, a picker drops bottles into a plastic tote, and the packer closes
a carton that leaves with a label on it. Nobody counts bottles one by one at each of those
steps: they move boxes.

Odoo calls such a box a **package**. Once packages are on, a transfer can move a whole
carton or pallet, the shelf knows which carton holds what, and the labels on the boxes
become barcodes that someone can scan. This chapter builds all of it on the depot: three
kinds of boxes, a pallet of two cartons received and stored in one piece, a sale shipped
in a carton, and a close look at how Odoo reads barcodes.

## Concepts

### Package, packaging, package type

Three words that sound alike and mean different things:

| Word | What it is | Example | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Package** | one physical box, with its own name and contents | carton CTN0000001 holding 12 bottles | Products → Packages (`stock.package`) |
| **Package type** | a kind of box: size, weights, name prefix | Carton, 400 × 300 × 300 mm, up to 20 kg | Configuration → Package Types |
| **Packaging** | a unit a product is sold or bought in | Pack of 6, which contains 6 Units | Configuration → Units & Packagings |

A package is a record you can open, move and print. A packaging is just a unit of
measure, as you saw in *The Warehouse Map*: it says how many units, not which box. The
two meet in one field: a packaging can name the **package type** its goods travel in.

Packages hang on stock in two places. A **quant** says "12 bottles of oil in LFD/Stock,
in package CTN0000001". A **move line** says where the goods come from (**Source
Package**) and where they go (**Destination Package**). Put in Pack fills the second one.

### Containers

A package can sit in another package. Odoo calls the outer one the **Container**
(`parent_package_id`): two cartons on a pallet are two packages whose container is the
pallet. The pallet itself holds no goods directly; its content is the content of its
cartons. Names show the nesting as `PAL0000001 > CTN0000001`.

### When a package moves in one piece

When Odoo reserves goods that sit in a package, it checks whether the transfer takes the
package's whole content. If it does, the move line keeps the package as its destination
too, and the carton arrives as a carton. If all of a container's packages move, the
container moves with them. Both rules have one exception, and it is the most important
switch in this chapter: **the package type's Package Use**.

- **Disposable Box** (the default): the box travels. A pallet stored as a pallet, a carton
  shipped as a carton.
- **Reusable Box (totes)**: the box is emptied at the next step. A picker's tote carries
  bottles to the packing table, the packer takes them out, and the tote goes back, empty,
  to be used again.

### Packagings on transfer lines

When a sales order line is in a packaging, say 2 × Pack of 6, the stock move is still in
the product's own unit, 12 Units. The packaging rides along in two more fields,
**Packaging** and **Packaging Quantity** (`packaging_uom_id`, `packaging_uom_qty`). They
do two jobs:

- **Choosing the box.** If the packaging names a package type, Put in Pack without the
  package-type question uses it.
- **Reserving.** A product category has **Reserve Packagings**: **Reserve Partial
  Packagings** (the default) reserves whatever is there; **Reserve Only Full Packagings**
  reserves only whole packs, so 25 bottles on hand cover 4 packs of 6, not 4 and a bit.

### Barcodes and nomenclatures

A barcode is just text. A **barcode nomenclature** decides what that text means. It is a
list of rules, each with a **Type** (Unit Product, Weighted Product, Location, Package,
Lot, and more), an **Encoding** and a **Barcode Pattern**. Odoo reads a scanned code
against the rules in order and stops at the first pattern that matches the start of the
code.

Odoo ships two:

- **Default Nomenclature**: five rules. Codes starting with `21` are weighed products
  (the weight is in the digits), `414` are locations, `PACK` are packages, `10` are lots,
  and anything else is a product.
- **Default GS1 Nomenclature**: the GS1 standard, used by most retail and logistics
  barcodes. A code is a chain of **application identifiers**: `(01)` a product's GTIN,
  `(10)` a lot, `(17)` an expiry date, `(30)` a quantity, `(00)` a shipping container's
  SSCC, and about 25 more.

Barcodes live on many records: the product (**Barcode**), each of its packagings
(**Packaging Barcodes**), locations, operation types, lots (their name), packages (their
name) and package types (**Barcode**).

### Labels

Every label is a report you print from a record. PDF labels go to an ordinary printer;
ZPL labels are text for a thermal label printer (Zebra).

| Label | Printed from | Formats |
|---|---|---|
| Product labels | product, or a transfer's **Print → Labels** | Dymo, 2 x 7 with price, 4 x 7 with price, 4 x 12, 4 x 12 with price, ZPL Labels, ZPL Labels with price |
| Lot/SN labels | lot, or **Print → Labels** on a transfer with lots | 4 x 12 or ZPL; one per lot/SN or one per unit |
| Package Barcode, Package Barcode with Contents | package | PDF, ZPL |
| Packages | transfer | PDF list of the transfer's packages |
| Location Barcode | location | PDF |
| Operation type | operation type | PDF, ZPL |

An operation type can also print by itself: on validation (delivery slip, return slip,
product labels, lot labels, package contents) and on Put in Pack (the package label, PDF
or ZPL).

### What the Barcode app adds

Everything above is Community. The **Barcode** app (Enterprise, the **Barcode Scanner**
setting) adds the scanning screens for receipts, transfers and counts, a choice of
nomenclature in the settings, and two fields that Community stores but never shows on a
form: **Package Use** on the package type and **Move Entire Packages** on the operation
type (which, in the Barcode app, shows packages instead of their products). The logic
behind Package Use runs in Community anyway, as you will see.

## Gotchas

- **Package Use and Move Entire Packages are not on any Community form.** Both fields
  exist and Package Use drives real logic, but only the Barcode app shows them. Set Package
  Use by import or code, and expect every type you create in the form to be disposable.
- **A reusable box never travels.** At the next step its goods are taken out, whether the
  box is a tote or a pallet. If your pallets come back to the vendor and you still want
  them stored whole, keep their type disposable.
- **Reload after switching packages on.** The web client can serve the transfer screen it
  cached before, without the Packages column and the Put in Pack button. One reload fixes
  it.
- **Custom prefixes are not packages to the Default Nomenclature.** Only codes starting
  with `PACK` are. CTN and PAL names parse as products until a rule says otherwise, and
  any product barcode starting with `21` parses as a weight.
- **A container's Package Transfers is empty.** The move lines name the cartons, not the
  pallet. Look at a carton, or at the transfers' package history.
- **Max Weight does not stop you.** Core Inventory stores it; nothing refuses an
  overloaded carton. The delivery modules read it for carrier packages.
