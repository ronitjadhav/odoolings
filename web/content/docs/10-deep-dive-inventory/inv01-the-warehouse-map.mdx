**Goal:** know what Odoo stores when it "has stock", where it stores it, and how a
quantity appears, by building LibreFleet's parts depot from nothing. · **Time:** ~2 h ·
**Checkpoint:** none, this deep dive writes no module until its code chapter. It builds a
database called `warehouse`.

## Why this matters

Every inventory question a client will ever ask you is one of these five:

- *Do we have it?* (a product, and whether Odoo counts it at all)
- *Where is it?* (a location)
- *Which site?* (a warehouse)
- *How did it get here?* (an operation type, and a transfer)
- *How many, exactly, right now?* (a quant)

This chapter teaches those five words with real records in a real database. Nothing
else in Inventory makes sense before them, and once you have them, every later chapter
is "the same five things, arranged differently". Routes move quants between locations.
Replenishment watches quants and creates transfers. Lots and packages are extra labels on
quants. Valuation puts a price on quants.

Chapter 25 of the journey touched this ground in one pass, inside a database full of
demo data. Here we go slowly, in an empty database, so you can see every record you
create and nothing you did not.

## Concepts

### Odoo does not store "stock". It stores quants

A **quant** is one row that says: *this product, in this location, this many*. That is
all stock is in Odoo: a table of quants. Twenty brake pad sets on the depot shelf is one
quant. Three of the same part in the service van is another quant. The same product can
have as many quants as the places it sits in.

<Mermaid
  label="A quant is the meeting point of one product and one location, carrying a quantity. Every stock report is a sum over quants."
  chart={`flowchart LR
    P["Product<br/>Brake Pad Set"] --> Q["Quant<br/>Brake Pad Set, LFD/Stock, 20"]
    L["Location<br/>LFD/Stock"] --> Q
    Q --> R["Reports<br/>On Hand, Forecast, Locations"]`}
/>

You never type a quant by hand. Quants change in exactly one way: a **transfer** is
validated, and its moves add to the quant at the destination and take away from the
quant at the source. Keep that sentence; it is the whole mechanism, and the next chapter
is about it.

### Which products Odoo counts

Not every product has quants. Three settings on the product decide it, and they are all
on the product's first tab:

| Setting | What it means in plain words | Example in this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| **Product Type: Goods** | a physical thing | brake pads, oil, a tablet, rags |
| **Product Type: Service** | time or work, nothing to put on a shelf | an hour of labour |
| **Track Inventory** (only for Goods) | "count this one" | ticked for pads, oil and the tablet; unticked for rags |

A Goods product with Track Inventory **off** is still bought and received, but Odoo
does not keep a quant for it. It is used up and forgotten. Shop rags, screws, cleaning
spray: things nobody wants to count. In the database the box is the field
`is_storable`, which is why developers call such a product "storable".

Two more product fields belong to Inventory and sit on the same tab: **Unit** (the
"per Units" next to the prices) and, on the **Inventory** tab, a **Responsible** user,
**Weight** and **Volume**, and a **Customer Lead Time**. Tracking by lot or serial
number is also a product setting, but it is switched off in a fresh database; the
traceability chapter of this deep dive switches it on.

### Locations: a tree with seven kinds of branch

A **location** is a place stock can be. Locations form a tree: `LFD` contains
`LFD/Stock`, which contains `LFD/Stock/Shelf A`. The full path is the location's
**complete name**, and Odoo always shows it, so you can tell two "Shelf A" apart.

Each location has a **Location Type** (the field is called `usage`), and the type is
what decides how Odoo treats it:

| Location Type | What it is for | In the warehouse database |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | a real place inside your building, where quants live | `LFD/Stock`, your shelves |
| **Virtual** | a folder that groups other locations, holds nothing itself | `LFD`, the warehouse's top node |
| **Vendor** | where goods come *from* when you receive them | `Vendors` |
| **Customer** | where goods go *to* when you deliver | `Customers` |
| **Inventory Loss** | the other side of a count correction or a scrap | `Inventory adjustment` |
| **Production** | the other side of manufacturing: components go in, products come out | `Production` |
| **Transit** | goods on the road between two warehouses or two companies | `Inter-warehouse transit` (archived until you need it) |

Vendor, Customer, Inventory Loss, Production and Transit are often called **virtual
locations** in conversation, because nothing of yours is physically there. Odoo still
keeps quants in them, and that is the trick behind the whole system: when you receive
20 brake pads from a vendor, `Vendors` goes to **minus 20** and `LFD/Stock` goes to
**plus 20**. Stock never appears from nowhere; it always moves from one location to
another, and the sum over all locations stays zero. You will see the minus twenty on
screen in this chapter.

### A warehouse is a bundle

A **warehouse** is one site: a building, a yard, a van. Creating or renaming one does a
lot for you. Odoo generates:

- a view location named after the warehouse's **Short Name** (`LFD`), with `LFD/Stock`
  inside it, plus four more locations that stay archived until a multi-step setting
  needs them (`Input`, `Quality Control`, `Output`, `Packing Zone`);
- one **operation type** per door: Receipts, Delivery Orders, and Internal Transfers;
- a numbering sequence per operation type, prefixed with the Short Name: `LFD/IN/`,
  `LFD/OUT/`, `LFD/INT/`;
- the routes that connect them (the routes chapter of this deep dive opens that box).

The Short Name is at most five characters and it is everywhere: in every location path
and every transfer reference. Choose it once, before anything is received.

### Operation types: the doors of the warehouse

An **operation type** is a kind of transfer, with its own numbering and its own default
"from" and "to". A fresh warehouse has three:

| Operation type | Type of Operation (`code`) | From | To | Prefix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Receipts | Receipt (`incoming`) | Vendors | LFD/Stock | `LFD/IN/` |
| Delivery Orders | Delivery (`outgoing`) | LFD/Stock | Customers | `LFD/OUT/` |
| Internal Transfers | Internal Transfer (`internal`) | LFD/Stock | LFD/Stock | `LFD/INT/` |

The Inventory **Overview** is simply one card per operation type, with a button to open
its transfers. Later chapters add more doors: Pick, Pack, Storage, Manufacturing,
Dropship. They are all the same idea: a named kind of transfer with defaults.

### Units, and why a "Pack of 6" is also a unit

Every quantity has a **unit**. Brake pads are counted in Units, oil could be in litres,
labour in hours. Odoo 19 keeps one list for all of them, **Units & Packagings**, and the
list says how units relate: `Pack of 6` contains 6 Units, `L` contains 1,000 ml, `Days`
contains 8 Hours. A packaging (the box the vendor ships) is just a unit that contains
some other unit, so receiving 2 packs of 6 and receiving 12 units put the same quant on
the shelf. This chapter only looks at the list; the packages chapter of this deep dive
puts packagings on a product and on a transfer line.

## Gotchas

- **The Short Name is forever, in practice.** It is baked into every location path and
  every transfer number. Renaming it later renames the locations, but the old numbers
  (`WH/IN/00001`) stay on the documents already done. Name the warehouse before the
  first receipt.
- **Track Inventory off means no quant, not quantity zero.** The Stock report simply has
  no row for the product, which surprises people who expected to see 0. If a client
  cannot find a product in stock reports, check the box first.
- **Vendors and Customers are negative on purpose.** A report over *all* locations sums
  to zero. "Internal Locations" is the filter that gives the number people usually mean.
- **The first Receipts list is filtered on Ready.** A draft or a done receipt is hidden
  behind that filter. Remove the facet before you conclude a transfer vanished.
- **A new product from Inventory's Products menu has Track Inventory ticked already.**
  That menu's action sets the default; the field itself defaults to off, so the same form
  opened from another app's Products menu starts unticked. Which menu you create a
  product from changes a default that decides whether it is ever counted.
