## Why this matters

LibreFleet's workshop does not only buy brake pads, it assembles them: a friction pad
bonded to a backing plate. The journey touched manufacturing once, in chapter 26, with
demo data. Here the point is different: to the warehouse, a manufacturing order is
nothing new. Components leave a location, a finished product arrives in another, and
the thing in the middle is a location called Production. Every word from the last three
chapters applies: quants, moves, rules, pushes and pulls.

Once you see that, the manufacturing settings stop being mysterious. "Three steps" is
two more operation types and two more locations. A "kit" is a bill of materials that
explodes when a transfer is confirmed. "Manufacture to Resupply" is one more rule in the
warehouse's list. This chapter builds each of those on the depot you already have.

## Concepts

### What Manufacturing adds to the warehouse

Installing the app (its module is `mrp`) adds four things you can see from Inventory:

- a **Manufacture** route, global and warehouse-selectable like Buy, with one rule per
  warehouse, *Stock (Production)*, of action **Manufacture**;
- a **Manufacturing** operation type (prefix `MO`), from Stock to Stock, with the
  **Production** location doing the work in between;
- on the warehouse form, a **Resupply** section: **Manufacture to Resupply** (is the
  Manufacture route on for this warehouse) and a **Manufacture** choice of one, two or
  three steps;
- a **Manufacturing** app with its own menus: Operations (Manufacturing Orders, Work
  Orders, Unbuild Orders, Scrap), Products (Products, Bills of Materials), Reporting,
  Configuration.

The Production location was there all along, archived-looking but active, from the
first chapter's location list. It is a *Production* type location: quants in it are
allowed to go negative, the same trick as Vendors and Customers.

### A bill of materials is a recipe

A **bill of materials** (`mrp.bom`) says what goes into one quantity of a product. The
form has:

| Field | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **Product** and **Quantity** | the recipe makes this many of this product |
| **BoM Type** | *Manufacture this product*: a manufacturing order consumes the components. *Kit*: no order, the components replace the product wherever it is moved |
| **Components** | one line per component, with its quantity for the BoM quantity |
| **Flexible Consumption** | whether a worker may consume more or less than the recipe: *Allowed*, *Allowed with warning* (the default), *Blocked* |
| **Operation Type** | which Manufacturing type to use, when a company has several |
| **Manuf. Lead Time** (`produce_delay`) | days to make it; the replenishment chapter adds it to the others |
| **Days to prepare Manufacturing Order** (`days_to_prepare_mo`) | how early to create the order so components can be bought first; **Compute** fills it from the components' lead times |
| **Batch Size** | split big orders into several of this size |

Two settings change the form: **Work Orders** (`group_mrp_routings`) adds an Operations
tab for work centers, which this deep dive leaves off, and **By-Products**
(`group_mrp_byproducts`) adds a tab for things the process produces besides the main
product, sawdust and offcuts, shown as extra finished moves.

### A manufacturing order is three moves in disguise

A **manufacturing order** (`mrp.production`) takes a BoM and a quantity and creates:

- one **component move** per BoM line (`move_raw_ids`): from the components location
  to **Production**;
- one **finished move** (`move_finished_ids`): from **Production** to the finished
  products location.

Validating the order is validating those moves. The components' quants go down, the
Production quant goes up and then down again, the finished product's quant goes up.
The Moves History report shows them next to receipts and deliveries, with `LFD/MO/00001`
as the reference.

The order has its own states: **Draft**, **Confirmed**, **In Progress**, **To Close**,
**Done**, **Cancelled**. Beside them sit two helper fields: **MO Readiness**
(`reservation_state`, *Waiting* or *Ready*, whether the components are reserved) and
**Component Status** (`components_availability`, *Available*, *Not Available*, or an
expected date). The buttons follow: **Confirm** reserves the components, **Produce All**
sets the produced quantity to the whole order and finishes it in one click, **Start**
only marks it In Progress, **Unreserve** gives the components back. A done order grows
an **Unbuild** button, which reverses it: the product goes back to components. The
operation type carries one manufacturing-specific option, *Create New Lots/Serial Numbers
for Components* (`use_create_components_lots`), for the traceability chapter.

### One step, two steps, three steps

The warehouse's **Manufacture** choice does for production what Incoming and Outgoing
Shipments did for receipts and deliveries:

| Manufacture | What a manufacturing order does | Locations woken up | Operation types woken up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manufacture (1 step)** | consumes from Stock, delivers to Stock | | |
| **Pick components then manufacture (2 steps)** | a **Pick Components** transfer brings parts from Stock to **Pre-Production**; the order consumes from there and delivers to Stock | Pre-Production | Pick Components (`PC`) |
| **Pick components, manufacture, then store products (3 steps)** | as above, and the order delivers to **Post-Production**; a **Store Finished Product** transfer carries the product to Stock | Pre-Production, Post-Production | Pick Components, Store Finished Product (`SFP`) |

Three steps generate one route with three rules, in the pattern you know from the
delivery route: a pull from Stock to Pre-Production (*Take From Stock*, Pick
Components), a pull from Pre-Production to Production (*Trigger Another Rule*,
Manufacturing), and a push from Post-Production to Stock (Store Finished Product).
Confirming an order pulls the Pick Components transfer at once; marking it done pushes
the Store Finished Product transfer.

### Kits: a bill of materials that explodes

A BoM of type **Kit** (`phantom` in the database) never has a manufacturing order. The
kit product is never on a shelf either: its on-hand quantity is computed from its
components. Instead, whenever a transfer line for the kit is **confirmed**, Odoo replaces
it with one line per component, each remembering which BoM line it came from
(`bom_line_id`), and the transfer flags itself `has_kits`. Sell a Brake Service Kit and
the Pick lists one brake pad set and two bottles of oil, both captioned with the kit's
name. The same happens when a kit is a component of another BoM. For LibreFleet this is
the service bundle: price one thing, ship its parts.

### Manufacture to Resupply, and an order that makes its own product

The Manufacture route is warehouse-selectable only, exactly like Buy: a product is
"manufacturable" by having a BoM, not by a route box. Put **Replenish on Order (MTO)**
on a product that has a BoM, and confirming a sale creates a Pick that *Triggers Another
Rule* at Stock; the rule that answers is *Stock (Production)*, which creates a
manufacturing order with the sales order as its **Source**. In a three-step warehouse
the whole chain is:

<Mermaid
  label="A sales order for a made-to-order product pulls a waiting Pick, which pulls a manufacturing order, which pulls a Pick Components transfer; doing each one pushes or wakes the next, until the delivery."
  chart={`flowchart TD
    SO["Sales order confirmed:<br/>need 2 pad sets in Customers"]
    SO -- "pull (MTO rule)" --> PICK["Pick, Waiting<br/>LFD/Stock to Packing Zone"]
    PICK -- "need 2 pad sets in LFD/Stock" --> MO["Manufacturing order, Confirmed<br/>Pre-Production to Post-Production"]
    MO -- "need components in Pre-Production" --> PC["Pick Components, Ready<br/>LFD/Stock to Pre-Production"]
    PC -- "validate: order turns Ready" --> MO2["Produce All"]
    MO2 -- "done, push" --> SFP["Store Finished Product<br/>Post-Production to LFD/Stock"]
    SFP -- "validate: Pick wakes up" --> PICK2["Pick, Pack, Delivery"]`}
/>

The **Allocation Report for Manufacturing Orders** setting (`group_mrp_reception_report`)
adds a report on a done order that says which customer orders the produced quantity can
serve, the manufacturing twin of the receipts' reception report.

<Callout type="info" title="Official docs">
  [Bill of materials](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/basic_setup/bill_configuration.html),
  [Three-step manufacturing](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/basic_setup/three_step_manufacturing.html)
  and [Use kits](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/advanced_configuration/kit_shipping.html)

</Callout>

## Gotchas

- **Produce All is Validate.** It sets the produced quantity to the whole order and marks
  it done in one click; there is no second confirmation. **Start** is the gentle button:
  it only moves the order to In Progress.
- **In three steps, Confirm does not reserve.** The components are in Stock and the
  order consumes from Pre-Production, so Consumed stays at 0 until the Pick Components
  transfer is validated. New users see "Available" in Component Status and wonder why
  nothing is reserved: Available is a forecast, Consumed is the fact.
- **A kit explodes on confirm, so a Draft shows the kit and a Ready shows the parts.** If
  you need to change the kit quantity, do it in Draft. After Mark as Todo there is no kit
  line to edit, only parts.
- **The Manufacture route is not on the product.** Like Buy, it is a warehouse route.
  A product is manufacturable because it has a bill of materials; the route box you tick
  on the product is Replenish on Order, which decides *when*, not *how*.
- **Changing the steps does not move anything already in Pre-Production or
  Post-Production.** Finish or cancel the open orders before switching, exactly as for
  the shipment steps.
- **The kit product's on-hand is computed, never counted.** Reporting shows a quantity for
  it (how many kits the components would make), but there is no quant and a stock count
  cannot touch it.
