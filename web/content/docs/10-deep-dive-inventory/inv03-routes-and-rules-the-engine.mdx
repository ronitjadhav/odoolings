## Why this matters

Until now every transfer in the depot was one you typed. Real warehouses are the other
way round: most transfers are created by Odoo, because a sales order was confirmed, a
receipt was validated, a minimum was crossed. The machine that creates them is a small
set of **routes** and **rules**, and almost every "where did this transfer come from?"
ticket an integrator gets is answered by reading one rule.

This chapter switches the engine on, reads what it generates, and then makes it run end
to end: one sales order for a product you do not have in stock, and Odoo buys it,
receives it, stores it, picks it, packs it and ships it, creating six documents you never
touched. Chapter 25 of the journey showed a two-step receipt in passing. Here you will see
every rule behind every step, in the simplest words we have.

## Concepts

### A rule is one sentence, a route is a list of them

A **rule** (`stock.rule`) is one instruction of the form *when a product is needed in
Customers, create a Delivery Order from LFD/Stock*. Odoo even writes the sentence for you,
on the rule's form. A **route** (`stock.route`) is a named list of rules, plus the places
where the route is allowed to apply. The engine is nothing more than: a need appears
somewhere, Odoo finds the rule whose destination is that somewhere, the rule creates a
transfer, and if that transfer needs stock it does not have, a new need appears one
location upstream.

<Mermaid
  label="A route is a named list of rules; a rule says, for one destination, which operation type moves goods there from where."
  chart={`flowchart LR
    R["Route<br/>Deliver in 3 steps (pick + pack + ship)"]
    R --> A["Rule: Pull From<br/>LFD/Stock to Customers<br/>via Pick"]
    R --> B["Rule: Push To<br/>LFD/Packing Zone to LFD/Output<br/>via Pack"]
    R --> C["Rule: Push To<br/>LFD/Output to Customers<br/>via Delivery Orders"]`}

### Four kinds of rule, two directions

The **Action** field is the rule's kind. Two of them come with Inventory, two arrive
with other apps:

| Action | Stored as | In plain words | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pull From** | `pull` | "goods are needed *here*: fetch them from *there*" | a need appears at the destination (an order, a reordering rule, another rule) |
| **Push To** | `push` | "goods arrived *here*: carry them on to *there*" | a transfer into the source location is **validated** |
| **Buy** | `buy` | a pull that creates a purchase order instead of a transfer | a need appears at the destination and the product has a vendor |
| **Manufacture** | `manufacture` | a pull that creates a manufacturing order | a need appears and the product has a bill of materials |

Pull works backwards from the customer; push works forwards from the dock. Odoo 19's
generated routes use both: one pull to get goods out of stock, then pushes to carry them
to the door. That matters for one practical reason you will see in the hands-on: in a
multi-step warehouse, the chain **starts at the Pick**, and a Delivery Order you type by
hand is a dead end.

### Supply Method: where the pull gets its goods

A pull rule also says how to satisfy the need it serves, in **Supply Method**
(`procure_method`):

| Supply Method | Stored as | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Take From Stock** | `make_to_stock` | reserve from whatever is in the source location; wait if there is nothing |
| **Trigger Another Rule** | `make_to_order` | do not touch existing stock: create a *new need* in the source location, so another rule (a pull, a Buy, a Manufacture) brings exactly this quantity |
| **Take From Stock, if unavailable, Trigger Another Rule** | `mts_else_mto` | the first, falling back to the second |

**Trigger Another Rule** is what people call **make to order**, or **MTO**. The
`Replenish on Order (MTO)` route is one rule with that supply method, archived in a fresh
database until you switch it on in the settings.

### The rest of the rule form

- **Operation Type**: which kind of transfer the rule creates, and therefore its number
  prefix and default locations.
- **Source Location** and **Destination Location**: the rule's "from" and "to". A move
  created by a pull takes its destination from the operation type, unless *Destination
  location origin from rule* (`location_dest_from_rule`) is ticked; the place the goods
  are ultimately heading for is kept apart on the move as **Final Location**
  (`location_final_id`), which is how a Pick knows it is really going to Customers.
- **Lead Time** (`delay`): days added to the scheduled date of the transfer this rule
  creates. The replenishment chapter adds these up.
- **Partner Address**: forces the transfer's partner, for drop-shipping style routes.
- **Cancel Next Move** (`propagate_cancel`): when this transfer is cancelled, cancel
  the one it feeds.
- **Automatic Move** (`auto`, push rules only): *Manual Operation* creates the next
  transfer and waits for a human; *Automatic No Step Added* validates it immediately.
- **Applicability** (`push_domain`, push rules only): a filter, so a push can apply to
  some moves and not others.

### Where a route is allowed to apply

A route only does something where it is **selectable**. The route form's *Applicable On*
boxes are the switches: **Product Categories**, **Products**, **Package Type**,
**Warehouses**, and with Sales installed, **Sales Order Lines**; with delivery methods,
**Shipping Methods**. When a need appears, Odoo gathers the routes from the product, then
its category, then the warehouse, and picks the first rule (by **sequence**) whose
destination matches. The generated warehouse routes are warehouse-selectable and ticked
on your warehouse, which is why they apply to everything without being on any product.

### The warehouse writes the routes for you

You rarely write routes by hand. The warehouse form has two radio groups, **Incoming
Shipments** and **Outgoing Shipments**, each with one, two or three steps, and saving
them regenerates the warehouse's routes:

| Setting | Route Odoo names | Rules it holds | Locations it wakes up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receive and Store (1 step) | Receive in 1 step (stock) | pull Vendors to Stock | |
| Receive then Store (2 steps) | Receive in 2 steps (input + stock) | pull Vendors to Input, **push** Input to Stock (Storage) | Input |
| Receive, Quality Control, then Store (3 steps) | Receive in 3 steps (input + quality + stock) | pull, push to Quality Control, push to Stock | Input, Quality Control |
| Deliver (1 step) | Deliver in 1 step (ship) | pull Stock to Customers (Delivery Orders) | |
| Pick then Deliver (2 steps) | Deliver in 2 steps (pick + ship) | pull Stock to Output (Pick), **push** Output to Customers | Output |
| Pick, Pack, then Deliver (3 steps) | Deliver in 3 steps (pick + pack + ship) | pull Stock to Packing Zone (Pick), push to Output (Pack), push to Customers (Delivery Orders) | Packing Zone, Output |

Each extra step also wakes up an operation type (Storage, Quality Control, Pick, Pack)
and a **Cross Dock** type from Input straight to Output, for goods that should not be
shelved at all. Odoo renames the existing routes in place and archives the rules it no
longer needs, so your Receipts and Delivery Orders operation types keep their numbers.

### How one order becomes six documents

Put the pieces together for a product that is **Replenish on Order** and has a vendor,
in a warehouse that receives in two steps and delivers in three:

<Mermaid
  label="Confirming a sales order pulls a Pick with Trigger Another Rule, which pulls a purchase order through the Buy rule; validating each transfer then pushes the next one until the delivery."
  chart={`flowchart TD
    SO["Sales order confirmed:<br/>need 1 tablet in Customers"]
    SO -- "pull (MTO rule)" --> PICK["Pick, Waiting<br/>LFD/Stock to Packing Zone"]
    PICK -- "need 1 tablet in LFD/Stock" --> BUY["Buy rule:<br/>purchase order P00001"]
    BUY -- "confirm" --> IN["Receipt, Ready<br/>Vendors to LFD/Input"]
    IN -- "validate, push" --> STOR["Storage<br/>LFD/Input to LFD/Stock"]
    STOR -- "validate: Pick wakes up" --> PICK2["Pick, Ready"]
    PICK2 -- "validate, push" --> PACK["Pack<br/>Packing Zone to Output"]
    PACK -- "validate, push" --> OUT["Delivery Order<br/>Output to Customers"]`}
/>

Two pulls happen at confirmation and go backwards (the Pick, then the purchase order).
Everything after that is a push, and pushes happen one at a time, each when the
previous transfer is **validated**. So right after the order is confirmed you see one
Pick and one request for quotation, not the whole chain. The links between the moves are
stored on the moves themselves (`move_orig_ids` and `move_dest_ids`), which is what the
**Next Transfer** button follows, and what makes the Pick turn Ready the moment the
Storage is done.

### Replenish on Order, and the Buy route

**Replenish on Order (MTO)** is a global route, archived until you tick the setting of
the same name. Once active, it is product-selectable: tick it on a product's Inventory
tab and every sale of that product creates a dedicated chain instead of taking from the
shelf. Its one rule is *Trigger Another Rule* from Stock to Customers.

The **Buy** route arrives with Purchase. In Odoo 19 it is **not** a box on the product:
it is a warehouse route, and its rule, *Stock (Buy)*, fires for any product that has a
vendor on its Purchase tab whenever a need reaches `LFD/Stock` that nothing else serves.
A need from a *Trigger Another Rule* pull is exactly that. No vendor, no purchase order:
the need simply stays open, which is a classic support ticket.

## Gotchas

- **In a multi-step warehouse, start at the Pick.** A Delivery Order you create by hand
  goes from Output to Customers and waits forever, because nothing feeds Output. The pull
  rule lives on the Pick type; the Pack and the delivery are pushed from it.
- **Pushes fire on Validate, not on Mark as Todo.** You will not see the Pack until the
  Pick is done. People look for "the missing transfers" right after confirming; they are
  not missing, they are not born yet.
- **MTO without a vendor is a need that never closes.** The Pick waits, no purchase order
  appears, and nothing complains. The Buy rule only fires for a product with a line on its
  Purchase tab. Check that tab first, the routes second.
- **The Buy route is on the warehouse in 19, not the product.** Older guides tell you to
  tick Buy on the product; in 19 that box does not exist. A product is "buyable" by having
  a vendor.
- **Rule names are frozen at creation.** The rules generated with the warehouse still
  say `WH:`; the ones generated after you renamed it say `LFD:`. Harmless, confusing in a
  list, and a hint to rename the warehouse before anything else.
- **A changed step setting renames routes and archives rules; it does not touch open
  transfers.** Transfers created under the old routing keep their old locations, so
  finish or cancel them first.
