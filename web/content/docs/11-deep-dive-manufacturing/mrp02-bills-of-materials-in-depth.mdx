**Goal:** write bills of materials for a product with variants, and predict which recipe
Odoo will use before it does. · **Time:** ~2 h · **Checkpoint:** none. It continues the
`factory` database.

## Why this matters

A real workshop rarely has one recipe per product. LibreFleet Works builds a **Van
Racking Unit** in two sizes: the small one holds two drawers, the large one three. The
large one exists in a heavy-duty build too, with more steel. Bolts arrive in packs of
six, and every drawer is itself made from a recipe.

Odoo can express all of that, and on a real project the hard question is never "how do
I type a recipe". It is "**why did this order use the wrong one?**" A manufacturing
order picks its bill of materials by itself, by a rule almost nobody reads. This chapter
builds the recipes, then makes Odoo change its mind in front of you without changing a
single recipe.

## Concepts

### The fields of a bill of materials, all of them

The previous chapter used the defaults. Here is every field a Community bill of materials
(BoM) has, and when you see it:

| Field | What it means | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | the product template this recipe makes | always |
| **Product Variant** | one variant only; empty means "every variant" | with the Variants setting |
| **Quantity** | how many the recipe makes; components are for this many. A recipe for 10 brackets lists the components of 10 | always |
| **Reference** | a short name to tell two recipes apart, like `L-HD` | always |
| **BoM Type** | *Manufacture this product*, or *Kit* (chapter 8); *Subcontracting* appears with its setting (chapter 10) | always |
| **Components** | one line each: product, quantity, **Unit** | always; Unit with Units of Measure |
| **Apply on Variants** (on a line) | this line only counts for these variants | optional column, with Variants |
| **Consumed in Operation** (on a line) | which step of the routing uses it | with Work Orders (chapter 5) |
| **Flexible Consumption** | may a worker consume more or less: *Allowed*, *Allowed with warning*, *Blocked* | Miscellaneous tab |
| **Manuf. Lead Time**, **Days to prepare Manufacturing Order** (with **Compute**) | days to make it, and how early to create the order | Miscellaneous tab; chapter 7 |
| **Batch Size** | split big needs into orders of this size (new in Odoo 19) | Miscellaneous tab; chapter 7 |
| **Manufacturing Readiness**, **Operation Type** | start when the first operation's parts are there; which Manufacturing door to use | with Work Orders, and with Multi-Step Routes |

A BoM is never deleted in a hurry: **archive** it (Action menu) and it stops being used
while every order that used it keeps its history.

### One recipe for every size, or one recipe per size

When a product has variants, you have two tools, and they combine:

- **A shared recipe with filtered lines.** Leave *Product Variant* empty, and mark the
  lines that only belong to some sizes with **Apply on Variants**. A line with nothing
  in that column is for every variant. The small racking unit gets the line "2 drawer
  modules (S)", the large one gets "3 drawer modules (L)", and both get the steel and the
  bolts.
- **A recipe for one variant.** Fill *Product Variant* and the recipe only serves that
  variant. LibreFleet's heavy-duty L is one.

### A recipe inside a recipe

A component can have its own bill of materials. The drawer module does: it is a component
of the racking unit and a product made from steel, slides and bolts. That makes the
racking unit's recipe **two levels deep**:

<Mermaid
  label="The Van Racking Unit recipe lists steel sheets, drawer modules and packs of bolts; the drawer module has its own recipe of a steel sheet, a slide pair and eight bolts."
  chart={`flowchart TD
    R["Van Racking Unit (S)"] --> S1["4 Steel Sheet 2mm"]
    R --> D["2 Drawer Module"]
    R --> B1["2 Pack of 6 Bolt M8"]
    D --> S2["1 Steel Sheet 2mm each"]
    D --> SL["1 Drawer Slide Pair each"]
    D --> B2["8 Bolt M8 each"]`}
/>

Read this carefully: an order for a racking unit **consumes drawer modules from stock**.
It does not make them. Making the drawers is a separate manufacturing order, created by
hand or by the planning rules of chapter 7. The second level is there for the BoM
Overview, the cost, and for planning to know what to make first.

### Units: a pack of six is a unit

Odoo 19 keeps every unit in one list, and each unit says what it contains: *Pack of 6*
contains 6 *Units*. A BoM line can count bolts in packs while the shelf counts them one
by one. Two packs on the recipe are twelve bolts out of stock. When you pick a unit on a
line, the dropdown shows what the quantity would be in the product's own unit, so you
can check the arithmetic before you choose.

### Which recipe wins

When an order is created for a product, Odoo looks for its BoM with one rule
(`_bom_find`). It takes every **active** BoM that is either for **this variant** or
**shared** (no variant), and sorts them:

| Order | Key | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **sequence**, smallest first | the order of the rows in the Bills of Materials list, once someone has dragged it |
| 2nd | variant before shared | a recipe for this exact variant beats the shared one |
| 3rd | oldest first | the BoM created first |

The first one wins. The trap is the first key. A new BoM is saved with **no sequence at
all** (empty, not zero), and an empty sequence sorts **after** every number. As long as
nobody touches the list, all your recipes tie on the first key and the variant-specific
recipe wins. The day someone drags a row in the Bills of Materials list, Odoo numbers
**every** row, and from then on the list order decides, before anything else. Same
recipes, same products, different result.

  [Bill of materials](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/manufacturing/basic_setup/bill_configuration.html)

  [Product variants](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/sales/sales/products_prices/products/variants.html)
  explains attributes. Neither spells out the sorting rule above: it lives in the code.
</Callout>

## Gotchas

- **An untouched list and a dragged list follow different rules.** With empty sequences,
  a variant's own recipe wins. After any drag, the row order wins. Teach the client this
  before they discover it on a Friday: "the BoM on top is the one used".
- **A recipe for a sub-assembly does not make the sub-assembly.** The racking unit's order
  consumes drawer modules from the shelf. With none there, it waits; it does not start a
  drawer order on its own unless planning is set up to (chapter 7).
- **An order keeps the recipe it was created with.** `LFW/MO/00002` stays on L-HD even if
  you reorder the list now. To move an existing draft to another recipe, change its
  **Bill of Material** field before confirming.
- **Apply on Variants is hidden by default.** Without the column you can create a line
  that applies to every size by mistake, and the small unit gets three drawers. Turn the
  column on in the picker before writing a recipe for a product with variants.
- **The unit list has every unit in it.** Odoo 19 has no unit categories, so the Unit
  dropdown on a BoM line offers litres and kilograms for bolts too. Read the converted
  quantity it shows next to each unit before you pick one.
