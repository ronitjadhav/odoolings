**Goal:** read any manufacturing order at a glance (where it is, what it waits for, what
it will consume) and know what each button does to it. · **Time:** ~2.5 h ·
**Checkpoint:** none. It continues the `factory` database.

## Why this matters

On the workshop floor nothing goes as the recipe says. Parts are missing when the order is
released. Half the batch is finished on Friday and the rest next week. A welder uses an
extra pack of bolts. A sheet gets bent and goes in the skip. A customer halves the order.

Odoo has a precise answer for each of those, and each answer leaves a trace: a state, a
backorder with a suffix, a warning someone confirmed, a scrap record, a line in the
chatter. When a client asks "why does this order say X", the answer is always one of
these traces. This chapter makes every one of them happen on purpose, on the draft order
the previous chapter left: `LFW/MO/00002`, one heavy-duty L racking unit.

## Concepts

### Six states, and what moves an order between them

<Mermaid
  label="A manufacturing order goes Draft, Confirmed, In Progress, To Close, Done; it can be cancelled until it is done. Producing part of it with a backorder finishes this order and starts a new confirmed one."
  chart={`stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Draft
    Draft --> Confirmed: Confirm
    Confirmed --> InProgress: Start, or a quantity is produced or consumed
    InProgress --> ToClose: everything produced
    ToClose --> Done: Produce All
    Confirmed --> Done: Produce All
    InProgress --> Done: Produce less, Create backorder
    Draft --> Cancelled: Cancel
    Confirmed --> Cancelled: Cancel
    InProgress --> Cancelled: Cancel`}
/>

| State | In the database | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | `draft` | being written; nothing reserved, quantities editable |
| **Confirmed** | `confirmed` | released: components reserved if they are there, quantity changed only through a wizard |
| **In Progress** | `progress` | someone pressed Start, or something was produced or consumed |
| **To Close** | `to_close` | everything is produced; only the final click is missing |
| **Done** | `done` | stock moved; the order is locked |
| **Cancelled** | `cancel` | dropped; reservations released, nothing moved |

The order passes through *To Close* even when you click Produce All in one go: the
chatter will show In Progress, then To Close, then Done, a second apart.

### Two fields that say what it waits for

| Field | Values | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| **MO Readiness** (`reservation_state`) | *Waiting*, *Ready*, *Waiting Another Operation* | are all the components reserved for this order? |
| **Component Status** (`components_availability`) | *Available*, *Not Available*, or an expected date (late ones in red) | in plain words, for the person planning the work |

Readiness changes **by itself**. A receipt of a missing component, or another order that
finishes the sub-assembly, reserves the parts for the waiting order without anyone
clicking. **Check availability** forces a new try; **Unreserve** gives everything back.

### The quantities on the form

- **Quantity** reads *producing / to produce*: `1.00 / 2.00` means one unit is being
  produced right now out of two. On a confirmed order the second number is a blue link
  that opens **Change Quantity To Produce**.
- **To Consume** is the recipe times the quantity. Once something is being produced it
  reads *for this unit / for the whole order*, like `6.00 / 12.00`.
- **Consumed** is what will be consumed when you produce. Before that, Odoo fills it with
  what it reserved. Its colour talks: **green** means it matches the recipe, **orange**
  means more than the recipe, **bold blue** means a person typed it (Odoo stops
  recalculating that line, the field `manual_consumption`).

### Producing less: backorders

Produce fewer units than the order asks and Odoo stops you with **You produced less than
the initial demand**:

- **Create backorder**: this order is finished for what you made, and a new confirmed
  order is created for the rest. Both get a suffix: the original becomes
  `LFW/MO/00002-001`, the rest `LFW/MO/00002-002`. A **Backorders** smart button links
  them.
- **No Backorder**: finish short; the rest is forgotten.
- **Discard**: go back to the order.

Whether you see the question at all is the Manufacturing operation type's **Create
Backorder** setting (*Ask*, *Always*, *Never*) from chapter 1.

### Consuming something else: the consumption warning

When the consumed quantities differ from the recipe, the bill of materials' **Flexible
Consumption** decides:

| Flexible Consumption | What happens at Produce |
|---|---|
| **Allowed** | nothing is asked |
| **Allowed with warning** (default) | **Consumption Warning** lists the differences: **Confirm** keeps them, **Set Quantities & Validate** resets them to the recipe and finishes, **Discard** goes back |
| **Blocked** | the same dialog without Confirm; only a Manufacturing Administrator sees **Force** |

### The Action menu, and the buttons that appear later

The gear next to the order's name holds the actions you use less often:

- **Split**: cut a draft or confirmed order into several (by count or by size);
- **Lock/Unlock**: a done order is locked so nobody edits its consumption by accident.
  The setting **Unlock Manufacturing Orders** makes orders open by default;
- **Scrap**: throw away a component or the product, linked to this order;
- **Plan based on Components Availability**: for work orders, chapter 6.

Select several orders in the list and the list's Action menu offers **Merge**: same
product and BoM, same state (draft or confirmed), same operation type, and no extra
lines. A done order grows an **Unbuild** button (chapter 8).

Two more traces of a living recipe: if the BoM changes after an order is confirmed, the
order shows that its bill of materials is outdated, with an **Update BoM** button. An
order created without a BoM, with components typed by hand, offers to **generate** a BoM
from them.

### The MO Overview

The **Overview** smart button opens a report of the order: every component with its
quantity, and columns you choose under **Display**. On an open order it shows
availability, replenishment and receipts. On a done order it shows **Unit Costs**, **MO
Costs**, **BoM Costs** and **Real Costs**, which is where chapter 11 reads what a racking
unit really cost.

## Hands-on

Same database, `factory`. On the shelf: 6 steel sheets, 16 slide pairs, 168 bolts and 4
drawer modules. The draft `LFW/MO/00002` makes one L racking unit with recipe L-HD (6
sheets, 3 drawers, 3 packs of bolts).

## Gotchas

- **Produce All resets numbers nobody typed.** It sets Consumed to the recipe for every
  line the user did not edit by hand. A script that writes `quantity` without
  `manual_consumption` gets its numbers silently replaced.
- **Read Component Status on a saved order.** After typing a quantity being produced, the
  unsaved form can show a stale status. Save, then read.
- **A backorder renames the original.** Reports and integrations that stored
  `LFW/MO/00002` will not find it any more: it is `LFW/MO/00002-001` now. Search by the
  start of the name, or by the backorder's link.
- **Cancelled is final.** A cancelled order cannot be set back to draft in Community.
  Duplicate it instead, or use the OCA's `mrp_production_back_to_draft`.
- **A scrap from an order does not touch the order's reservation.** The bent sheet came
  from the free stock on the shelf, and the order still had its 6. When stock is tight,
  the order that comes up short is the *next* one.
