**Goal:** know what Odoo needs before it can make anything, and what a manufacturing order
really does to your stock, by building LibreFleet's workshop from nothing. · **Time:**
~2 h · **Checkpoint:** none, this deep dive writes no module until its code chapter. It
builds a database called `factory`.

## Why this matters

LibreFleet does not only repair vans, it fits them out. In a corner of the depot sits a
small workshop, **LibreFleet Works**, that cuts steel, welds racks and bolts drawers
together, so a service van leaves with shelves made to measure. That workshop is a
factory, and Odoo's Manufacturing app is how you run one.

Every manufacturing question a client asks is one of these four:

- *What do we make, and from what?* (a bill of materials)
- *Where does it happen?* (the warehouse, and a location called Production)
- *Which document makes it?* (a manufacturing order)
- *What did it use up, and what came out?* (stock moves)

This chapter answers all four with real records in an empty database. Work centers,
planning, traceability and costing come later in this deep dive, and every one of them
hangs on these four answers.

The journey met manufacturing once, in
[chapter 26](/docs/04-odoo-business/26-manufacturing-bom-orders), inside a database full of
demo data. [Inventory 4](/docs/10-deep-dive-inventory/inv04-manufacturing-meets-the-warehouse)
looked at it from the warehouse's side. This deep dive stands on the workshop floor.

## Concepts

### Manufacturing sits on top of Inventory

You cannot make anything without stock to make it from, so the Manufacturing app (its
module is `mrp`) installs Inventory (`stock`) underneath it. Everything the Inventory deep
dive taught still holds here: stock is a table of quants, and a quant only changes when a
move is validated.

A **manufacturing order** is, to the warehouse, just a handful of moves with one special
location in the middle:

<Mermaid
  label="A manufacturing order moves each component from the stock location into the Production location, and moves the finished product from Production back into stock."
  chart={`flowchart LR
    S1["LFW/Stock<br/>steel sheet, slides, bolts"] -- "component moves" --> P["Production<br/>(a virtual location)"]
    P -- "finished move" --> S2["LFW/Stock<br/>drawer module"]`}
/>

**Production** is a location of type *Production*. Nothing physical is there; it is the
other side of the bargain. Components go in and stay there, so its quants grow. Finished
products come out of it, so its quants go below zero. The same trick as the Vendors
location on a receipt: stock never appears from nowhere, and the sum over all locations
stays zero.

### What the app adds

Installing Manufacturing adds these, all of which you will see in this chapter:

| What | Name in the database | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| a **Manufacturing** operation type | `stock.picking.type`, code `mrp_operation`, prefix `MO` | the "door" that manufacturing orders go through, with its own numbering, `LFW/MO/00001` |
| a **Production** location | `stock.location`, usage `production` | where components go in and finished products come out |
| a **Manufacture** route | `mrp.route_warehouse0_manufacture`, one rule per warehouse | lets a need for a product create a manufacturing order; the planning chapters use it |
| on the warehouse, **Manufacture to Resupply** and a choice of **one, two or three steps** | `manufacture_to_resupply`, `manufacture_steps` | whether this warehouse makes things, and whether components are picked first; one step until a later chapter |
| the **Manufacturing** app | menus, two access groups (User, Administrator) | where you work |
| a **bill of materials** model | `mrp.bom`, `mrp.bom.line` | the recipe |
| the **manufacturing order** model | `mrp.production` | the document that makes the product |

### The menus

The app's top menu is short, and it grows with the settings:

- **Operations**: Manufacturing Orders, Unbuild Orders, Scrap. Work Orders joins them
  once the Work Orders setting is on.
- **Products**: Products and Bills of Materials. Product Variants and Lots/Serial Numbers
  appear when their settings are on.
- **Configuration**: Settings. Work Centers and Operations appear with Work Orders.
- **Reporting** is missing on purpose: both of its reports (Work Orders analysis and
  Overall Equipment Effectiveness) are about work centers, so the menu only shows with
  the Work Orders setting.

A fifth menu, **Planning**, only exists in developer mode, where it offers *Procurement:
run scheduler*.

### The settings, one by one

**Manufacturing → Configuration → Settings** has two blocks. Every box, and the chapter
of this deep dive that uses it:

|---|---|---|

| **Work Order Dependencies** (shows under Work Orders once it is ticked) | an operation can wait for another one | chapter 5 |

### A recipe and an order

A **bill of materials** (BoM) is a recipe: *this quantity of this product is made from
these components*. Its form has a header and two tabs:

- **Product** and **Quantity**: the recipe makes this many. One drawer module, here.
- **BoM Type**: *Manufacture this product* (an order consumes the components) or *Kit*
  (no order ever; the kit is replaced by its parts wherever it moves).
- **Components**: one line per component, with the quantity for the BoM's quantity.
- **Miscellaneous**: **Flexible Consumption** (may a worker use more or less than the
  recipe says: *Allowed*, *Allowed with warning*, which is the default, or *Blocked*),
  **Manuf. Lead Time** and **Days to prepare Manufacturing Order** (for planning, with a
  **Compute** button), and **Batch Size**, new in Odoo 19, which splits big needs into
  orders of a fixed size.

A **manufacturing order** (MO) takes a BoM and a quantity and walks through states:
**Draft** (being written), **Confirmed** (components reserved if they are there), **In
Progress** (someone started), **To Close** (everything produced, waiting for the last
click), **Done**, or **Cancelled**. Two fields sit beside the state: **MO Readiness**
(are the components reserved, *Ready* or *Waiting*) and **Component Status** (in plain
words: *Available*, *Not Available*, or the date they are expected). The next two
chapters take the recipe and the order apart field by field.

total is what Acier Plus delivered. Nothing appeared and nothing vanished.

## Gotchas

- **Rename the warehouse before installing Manufacturing.** A rename rewrites the
  numbering of Receipts, Deliveries and the other Inventory doors, not the doors
  Manufacturing added. In a database where Manufacturing came first, the same rename
  left this:

  ```python
  >>> wh.write({"name": "LibreFleet Works", "code": "LFW"})
  True
  >>> wh.name, wh.lot_stock_id.complete_name, wh.manu_type_id.sequence_id.prefix
  ('LibreFleet Works', 'LFW/Stock', 'WH/MO/')
  ```

  The repair: developer mode, **Settings → Technical → Sequences & Identifiers →
  Sequences**, open *My Company Sequence production* (its name did not change either)
  and set **Prefix** to `LFW/MO/`. Do the same for the Pick Components and Store Finished
  Product sequences if you use more than one step.
- **Production's quants are not a mistake.** Positive components and a negative finished
  product in the Production location are the bookkeeping of every order. Filter on
  internal locations when you mean "our stock".
- **Consumed is filled in before anything is consumed.** On a confirmed order, the
  Consumed column shows the reserved quantity in green. Nothing has left the shelf until
  the order is done; it is a proposal you can change.
- **No Reporting menu does not mean no reports.** Reporting appears with the Work Orders
  setting. Until then, the manufacturing order list's pivot and graph views (the icons
  on the right of the search bar) are where the numbers are.
- **The product menu you start from matters.** Products created from Manufacturing's or
  Inventory's Products menu have Track Inventory ticked; the field itself defaults to
  off. A component created somewhere else and left unticked is never reserved and never
  counted: an order consumes it without anything on the shelf going down.
