**Goal:** be able to say what a warehouse is worth, which of two independent switches
decides that, and exactly which journal entry a delivery will post before you validate
it. · **Time:** ~2.5 h · **Checkpoint:** none, this part continues in the `functional`
demo database

## Why this matters

You have bought brake pads twice and manufactured four more. Ask your database what they
are worth and it says this:

```text
qty_available: 19.0 | standard_price: 0.0
```

Nineteen units, worth nothing. Meanwhile Odoo knows perfectly well that 630.00 of stock
came through the door, because it wrote that number down on every move as it happened.
And the accounting journal reserved for stock, the STJ you met in chapter 27, has never
had a single entry posted to it.

None of that is a bug. It is three separate mechanisms doing exactly what they were
configured to do, and a developer who cannot tell them apart cannot answer the question
this chapter exists for: **why is the P&L wrong?** That ticket is nearly always one of
these three, and they have different fixes.

This is also where Part 4 and Part 5 finally join. The delivery you are about to validate
has been sitting in your database since chapter 22.

## Concepts

### Two switches, and people conflate them constantly

Valuation is quantity times cost, and Odoo splits the decision in two. They are set in
the same place, on the product category, which is exactly why they get confused:

| Question | Field | Values |
|---|---|---|
| What is a unit worth? | `property_cost_method` | `standard`, `fifo`, `average` |
| When do the books hear about it? | `property_valuation` | `periodic`, `real_time` |

The first is a <Term k="costing method (`property_cost_method`)">costing</Term> decision. The second is an <Term k="inventory valuation (`property_valuation`)">accounting</Term> decision. You can
run AVCO costing and still post nothing to the ledger until year end, which is what the
demo database has been doing all along.

- **Standard Price**: a cost you type. Every movement is valued at that number until you
  change it. Predictable, and wrong the moment your supplier raises prices.
- **FIFO**: each receipt keeps its own cost, and outgoing moves consume the oldest layer
  first. Odoo tracks what is left of each receipt in `stock.move.remaining_value`.
- **AVCO**: one running average, recomputed on every receipt.

And for the second switch:

- **`periodic`**, labelled **Periodic (at closing)**: moves are still valued, but no
  journal entry is created. Somebody posts the change in stock manually at period end.
- **`real_time`**, labelled **Perpetual (at invoicing)**: every valued move posts its own
  journal entry as it happens.

<Callout type="warn" title="On Odoo 18 this differs">
  Both the stored values and the labels changed. Odoo 18 stores `manual_periodic` and
  calls the pair Manual/Automated; Odoo 19 stores `periodic` and calls them
  Periodic/Perpetual. Code that branches on `manual_periodic` is dead on 19, silently,
  which is the same shape of breakage as chapter 21's `type == 'product'`.
</Callout>

### Where the value actually lives, and what disappeared

If you have worked with Odoo 15 through 18, you know `stock.valuation.layer`: one row per
valuation event, the model every valuation report and every custom costing script was
built on.

**It does not exist in Odoo 19.** Confirm it yourself in the Hands-on. Valuation moved
onto the move:

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `stock.move.value` | what this movement was worth |
| `stock.move.is_valued` | whether it counts towards stock value at all |
| `stock.move.remaining_value` | what is left of this receipt, for FIFO |
| `stock.move.value_manual` and `value_justification` | a manual override and why |
| `stock.quant.value` | what is sitting in this location right now |

<Term k="valued move (`is_valued`)">`is_valued`</Term> is the one worth pausing on, because it encodes a real accounting idea.
Locations are either **inside** the company or outside it: `usage` of `internal` or
`transit` counts as inside, everything else (customers, vendors, production, inventory
loss) is outside. Value only changes when stock crosses that boundary. Move goods from
one shelf to another and nothing has happened economically, so the move is valued at
zero and `is_valued` is `False`. You will see exactly that on the two-step receipt from
chapter 25.

### The account routing, which also moved

Older Odoo hung a pair of interim accounts off the product category, Stock Input and
Stock Output. **Those fields are gone in 19.** The routing now reads like a sentence:

- the **product category** says which asset account holds stock, in
  `property_stock_valuation_account_id`
- each **location outside the company** says what value *becomes* when it arrives there,
  in `stock.location.valuation_account_id`

So Customers means cost of goods sold, Inventory Loss means a shrinkage expense,
Production means work in progress. One field per destination, and the entry writes itself:

<Mermaid
  label="Stock leaving for a customer credits the category's stock asset account and debits the account named on the Customers location, which is cost of goods sold"
  chart={`flowchart LR
    S["WH/Stock<br/>(inside the company)"] -- "delivery, 4 units" --> C["Customers<br/>valuation_account_id<br/>= 500000 COGS"]
    S -.-> A["category:<br/>110100 Stock Valuation"]
    A --> E1["credit 132.63"]
    C --> E2["debit 132.63"]
    E1 --> J["one STJ journal entry"]
    E2 --> J`}
/>

Core reads it in one `if`, and the symmetry is the whole design: whichever end of the
move names an account, the stock account takes the other side.

```python
if self.location_id.valuation_account_id:
    debit_acc = self.product_id._get_product_accounts()['stock_valuation']
    credit_acc = self.location_id.valuation_account_id
else:
    debit_acc = self.location_dest_id.valuation_account_id
    credit_acc = self.product_id._get_product_accounts()['stock_valuation']
```

The consequence catches everyone once: **a location with no account posts nothing.**
Not an error, not a warning. `_should_create_account_move` simply returns falsy and the
move completes with a value and no journal entry.

## Hands-on

Continuing in `functional`. Same two movements as the rest of Parts 4 and 5.

### Movement 1: run the flow

#### 1. Ask what you own, before changing anything

```console
$ docker compose exec odoo odoo shell -c /etc/odoo/odoo.conf -d functional --no-http
```

```python
>>> prod = env["product.product"].search([("product_tmpl_id.name", "=", "Brake Pad Set")], limit=1)
>>> prod.qty_available, prod.standard_price
(19.0, 0.0)
>>> prod.valuation, prod.cost_method
('periodic', 'standard')
>>> prod.categ_id.complete_name or "(none)"
'(none)'
```

Nineteen units at a standard cost of zero, because the product has **no category at
all**, and a category is what supplies a costing method, a valuation mode and a stock
account. Chapter 21 created this product through the interface without one, and nothing
has complained since.

Now ask the moves what they were worth:

```python
>>> stj = env["account.journal"].search([("code", "=", "STJ")], limit=1)
>>> env["account.move"].search_count([("journal_id", "=", stj.id)])
0
>>> moves = env["stock.move"].search([("product_id", "=", prod.id), ("state", "=", "done")])
>>> sum(moves.filtered("is_valued").mapped("value"))
630.0
```

**630.00 of stock has moved and the ledger has heard nothing.** Both numbers are correct:
`periodic` values every move and posts none of them. Hold on to the 630.00, it comes back.

#### 2. Confirm what is not there any more

Worth thirty seconds if you have ever written valuation code:

```python
>>> "stock.valuation.layer" in env
False
>>> "property_stock_account_input_categ_id" in env["product.category"]._fields
False
```

The layer model and the interim account fields are both gone. Anything you remember about
either is Odoo 18 knowledge.

#### 3. Give the product a category, and watch nothing happen

```python
>>> goods = env["product.category"].search([("name", "=", "Goods")], limit=1)
>>> prod.product_tmpl_id.categ_id = goods.id
>>> prod.invalidate_recordset()
>>> prod.valuation, prod.cost_method, prod.standard_price
('periodic', 'standard', 0.0)
```

Still zero. The category exists, but the demo's Goods category is itself on Standard
Price and Periodic, so the product inherited a costing method that says "the cost is
whatever you typed", and nobody has typed anything.

#### 4. Throw both switches

```python
>>> goods.write({"property_valuation": "real_time", "property_cost_method": "average"})
True
>>> prod.invalidate_recordset()
>>> prod.valuation, prod.cost_method, prod.standard_price
('real_time', 'average', 33.1578947368421)
```

The cost appeared out of nowhere, except it did not:

```python
>>> 630.0 / 19
33.15789473684211
```

That is the whole point of AVCO. The value was recorded on every move all along; asking
for an average is what makes Odoo go and read it. Nothing was imported and no cost was
typed.

#### 5. Tell the Customers location what stock becomes when it leaves

In the interface this is **Inventory → Configuration → Locations → Customers**, field
**Stock Valuation Account**. From the shell:

```python
>>> cust = env["stock.location"].search([("usage", "=", "customer")], limit=1)
>>> cogs = env["account.account"].search([("account_type", "=", "expense_direct_cost")], limit=1)
>>> cust.valuation_account_id = cogs.id
>>> cogs.code, cogs.name
('500000', 'Cost of Goods Sold')
```

#### 6. Validate the delivery that has been waiting since chapter 22

`WH/OUT/00044` is the delivery chapter 22 created when you confirmed S00025, and which
chapter 24's receipt quietly made reservable. It has been sitting at `assigned` ever
since. Open **Inventory → Deliveries**, find it, and **Validate**.

Odoo may ask whether to send the customer an SMS. Decline; the demo has no SMS credits
and it is not part of this lesson.

### Movement 2: read what it did

#### 7. The first stock journal entry in the database

```python
>>> pick = env["stock.picking"].search([("name", "=", "WH/OUT/00044")])
>>> pick.state
'done'
>>> mv = pick.move_ids[0]
>>> mv.quantity, mv.value
(4.0, 132.63)
>>> am = mv.account_move_id
>>> am.name, am.journal_id.name
('STJ/2026/08/0001', 'Inventory Valuation')
>>> for l in am.line_ids:
...     print(l.account_id.code, l.account_id.name, l.debit, l.credit)
...
110100 Stock Valuation 0.0 132.63
500000 Cost of Goods Sold 132.63 0.0
```

Four units at the 33.16 average is 132.63, and it lands as a balanced pair: the asset
account goes down, the expense account goes up. That is <Term k="COGS (cost of goods sold)">cost of goods sold</Term>, produced by
validating a transfer rather than by anything on the invoice, and it is the first entry
this journal has ever received.

Read it against chapter 27's rule and nothing is new. Value did not appear or vanish, it
moved: out of an asset, into an expense, because the goods left the company.

#### 8. Now look at the damage

```python
>>> acc = env["account.account"].search([("code", "=", "110100")], limit=1)
>>> lines = env["account.move.line"].search([("account_id", "=", acc.id), ("parent_state", "=", "posted")])
>>> sum(lines.mapped("debit")) - sum(lines.mapped("credit"))
-132.63
>>> prod.invalidate_recordset()
>>> prod.qty_available * prod.standard_price
497.3684210526316
```

Your warehouse holds 497.37 of brake pads. Your balance sheet says stock is worth
**minus 132.63**. The difference is 630.00, and you have seen that number before: it is
exactly the value that came in while the category was still `periodic`, when Odoo valued
every receipt and posted none of them.

This is the single most useful thing in the chapter. **Switching valuation mode is not
retroactive.** It does not go back and post the history, so a database that has traded
under Periodic and is flipped to Perpetual will report a negative stock asset until
somebody posts an opening entry for the stock already on hand. On a real migration that
entry is planned work, not a surprise, and the way you catch it is exactly the comparison
above: what the warehouse holds against what the account says.

#### 9. Why one of your moves is worth nothing

```python
>>> for m in env["stock.move"].search([("product_id", "=", prod.id), ("state", "=", "done")]):
...     print(m.reference, m.location_id.complete_name, "->", m.location_dest_id.complete_name, m.quantity, m.value, m.is_valued)
...
WH/IN/00007 Vendors -> WH/Stock 10.0 420.0 True
WH/IN/00008 Vendors -> WH/Input 5.0 210.0 True
WH/STOR/00001 WH/Input -> WH/Stock 5.0 0.0 False
WH/OUT/00044 WH/Stock -> Customers 4.0 132.63 True
WH/MO/00007 Production -> WH/Stock 4.0 0.0 True
```

`WH/STOR/00001` is the second leg of chapter 25's two-step receipt, and it is the only
one with `is_valued` of `False`. Both of its locations are inside the company, so nothing
economic happened: the goods were already yours before you moved them from Input to
Stock. Valuing that move would double-count the 210.00 the receipt above it already
recorded.

The manufacturing order is worth a second look too. `WH/MO/00007` produced four brake
pads and valued them at 0.00, because at the time it ran the product was on Standard
Price with a cost of zero. Manufacturing values its output at whatever the costing method
says the product is worth, and back then that was nothing.

## Gotchas

- **A product with no category values at zero, silently.** No warning at any point, on
  any screen. The category is where the costing method, the valuation mode and the stock
  account all come from, so a product without one is unpriceable by construction. This
  chapter's own product had gone nine chapters that way.
- **`stock.valuation.layer` does not exist in Odoo 19.** Value lives on `stock.move.value`.
  Every valuation script, report and custom costing module written for 15 to 18 is built
  on a model that is no longer there, which makes this one of the more expensive parts of
  an 18 to 19 migration.
- **A location with no valuation account posts nothing, and says nothing.**
  `_should_create_account_move` needs an account on one end or the other. Set the category
  to Perpetual, forget the location, and you get moves that are valued, a stock account
  that never changes, and no error to tell you.
- **Switching valuation mode is not retroactive.** History posted under Periodic stays
  unposted forever. Flipping a live database to Perpetual without an opening entry leaves
  the stock account reporting a negative asset, exactly as it does here.
- **Internal transfers are valued at zero on purpose.** `is_valued` is `False` whenever
  both ends are inside the company, and treating that as a missing value is a
  double-counting bug waiting to happen.
- **Changing the costing method rewrites the cost.** Moving to AVCO recomputed
  `standard_price` from the movement history the moment it was saved. On a live database
  that is a repricing of everything you own, which is why the field is not something to
  try out on production and see.
