**Goal:** take a confirmed order all the way to settled cash, and learn why "paid" is a
summary of something else rather than a fact of its own. · **Time:** ~2.5 h ·
**Checkpoint:** none, this part continues in the `functional` demo database

## Why this matters

This is the chapter that closes the loop. Chapter 22 left you with a confirmed order and
`invoice_status = 'to invoice'`; chapter 27 taught you that an invoice is a journal entry.
Now you connect them and follow the money out the other side.

The developer payoff is a specific correction. Everyone reads `payment_state == 'paid'`
and assumes that is where the truth lives. It is not: `payment_state` is *computed*, and
what it summarises is **reconciliation**, the act of matching a debit against a credit
until nothing is left over. Code that writes or trusts `payment_state` without
understanding reconciliation is the source of a whole genre of bug where the books look
right and are not.

## Concepts

### Invoicing is a wizard, not a button on the order

The **Create Invoice** button does not create the invoice itself. It opens a
`sale.advance.payment.inv` wizard, and the wizard decides what to bill: everything
deliverable, a down payment, a percentage. That matters the first time you try to
automate invoicing, because `sale.order._create_invoices` is a **private** method and
cannot be called over RPC. The wizard is the supported path in and out.

Whatever it creates arrives in **draft**. Chapter 27's rule holds: a draft invoice has no
number and no accounting effect at all. Posting is the event.

### The three-line invoice

The invoice you are about to post has three `account.move.line` children, and reading
them is the whole of accounts receivable:

| Line | Account | Side |
|---|---|---|
| the product | Product Sales | credit (income earned) |
| the tax | Tax Received | credit (tax you owe onward) |
| the total | Account Receivable | debit (what the customer owes you) |

The receivable equals the other two added together, because the entry balances.
Chapter 29 takes the tax line apart properly.

### A payment is also a journal entry

Registering a payment creates an `account.payment` record **and** an `account.move` with
`move_type = 'entry'`, in a bank journal. Chapter 27's duality again: there is no special
payment table holding money, just another balanced entry. Its two lines are a debit to
**Outstanding Receipts** (cash is on its way in) and a credit to **Account Receivable**
(the customer owes less now).

Notice what has *not* happened yet: the invoice's receivable line and the payment's
receivable line are two separate lines that happen to be on the same account. Something
has to declare that they cancel out.

### Reconciliation is that declaration

<Term k="reconciliation (`account.partial.reconcile`)">`account.partial.reconcile`</Term> is a record saying "this much of that debit is matched by
this much of that credit". One payment against one invoice creates one of them. Two
instalments create two, and their amounts sum to the invoice total.

<Mermaid
  label="Two partial reconciliation records, 200.00 and 163.40, match the invoice's single receivable debit until the residual reaches zero and payment_state computes to paid"
  chart={`flowchart LR
    subgraph inv["invoice INV/2026/00010"]
      R["Account Receivable<br/>debit 363.40"]
    end
    subgraph p1["payment PBNK1/.../00001"]

    end
    subgraph p2["payment PBNK1/.../00002"]
      C2["Account Receivable<br/>credit 163.40"]
    end
    R -- "partial.reconcile 200.00" --- C1
    R -- "partial.reconcile 163.40" --- C2
    R -.-> S["residual 0.00<br/>reconciled = True<br/>payment_state = paid"]`}
/>

Two fields describe the result, and the difference between them is worth holding on to:

- **`amount_residual`** on the move: how much is still unmatched, in currency.
- **`reconciled`** on the line: a boolean, true once that line is fully matched.
- **<Term k="payment_state">`payment_state`</Term>** on the move: the computed summary, one of `not_paid`,
  `partial`, `in_payment`, `paid`, `reversed`, `blocked` (plus a legacy value).

`payment_state` is derived from the first two. That is the direction of causation, and
getting it backwards is the bug this chapter is trying to prevent.

### Payment terms decide *when*, not *whether*

An <Term k="payment term (`account.payment.term`)">`account.payment.term`</Term> (the demo ships Immediate Payment, 15 Days, 30 Days, End of
Following Month and others) sets the due date and can split one invoice into several
instalments with their own dates. It changes nothing about the accounts used. Overdue is
a question about dates; unpaid is a question about reconciliation.

## Hands-on

Continuing in `functional`, with the confirmed order from chapter 22. First you run the
invoice through draft, partial and paid; then you read what each step actually posted.

<Steps>

<Step>
### Invoice the order

Snapshot, then open your confirmed order and press **Create Invoice**. In the wizard,
keep **Regular invoice** and confirm. You land on a **draft** invoice: no number in the
title, and nothing has hit the books.

![A draft customer invoice: the title reads Draft, no invoice number anywhere on the page](/screens/ch28-draft-invoice.jpg)

Press **Confirm** to post it.

![The same invoice posted and numbered, Amount Due 276.00, nothing paid yet](/screens/ch28-posted-invoice-not-paid.jpg)

</Step>

<Step>
### Pay part of it

On the posted invoice, press **Register Payment**. Change the amount to **200.00** (the
invoice is 363.40) and confirm. The invoice does not say Paid.

![A PARTIAL ribbon across the invoice, Paid 200.00 against a 276.00 total, Amount Due 76.00](/screens/ch28-invoice-partial.jpg)

</Step>

<Step>
### Pay the rest

Press **Register Payment** again. Leave the amount alone: it already defaults to what is
still owed. Confirm.

![A PAID ribbon across the invoice, two payment lines totalling the invoice amount, Amount Due 0.00](/screens/ch28-invoice-paid.jpg)

</Step>

<Step>
### What invoicing created

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   INV/2026/00010           move_type=out_invoice  state=posted  amount_total=363.4  payment_state=not_paid
account.move.line   +3
      new   Brake Pad Set (Front)    account_id=Product Sales  credit=316.0
      new   15%                      account_id=Tax Received  credit=47.4
      new   S00025 - INV/2026/00010  account_id=Account Receivable  debit=363.4
sale.order          ~
      chg   S00025                   invoice_status: to invoice -> invoiced
```

There are the three lines, and 316.00 plus 47.40 is exactly the 363.40 debited to the
customer. The order's `invoice_status` moved to `invoiced`, closing the loop chapter 22
opened. Your numbers will differ.

Confirm the draft-versus-posted rule from chapter 27 still holds:

```python
>>> inv.name, inv.state, inv.move_type      # created by the wizard, still draft
(False, 'draft', 'out_invoice')
>>> inv.name, inv.state, inv.payment_state  # after Confirm
('INV/2026/00010', 'posted', 'not_paid')
>>> inv.amount_total, inv.amount_residual
(363.4, 363.4)
```

Posted, numbered, and the entire amount still outstanding.

</Step>

<Step>
### What a partial payment created

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   PBNK1/2026/00001         move_type=entry  state=posted  amount_total=200.0  payment_state=not_paid
      chg   INV/2026/00010           payment_state: not_paid -> partial
account.move.line   +2
      new   Manual Payment: INV/2026/00010 account_id=Outstanding Receipts  debit=200.0
      new   Manual Payment: INV/2026/00010 account_id=Account Receivable  credit=200.0  reconciled=True
account.payment     +1
      new   PBNK1/2026/00001         state=in_process  amount=200.0  payment_type=inbound
```

Read the first line again: `move_type=entry`. **The payment is a journal entry**, in the
bank journal, numbered by that journal's sequence. And its receivable credit line comes
out already `reconciled=True`, because all 200.00 of it was matched immediately.

The invoice is now `partial`:

```python
>>> inv.payment_state, inv.amount_residual   # after paying 200.00 of 363.40
('partial', 163.4)
```

</Step>

<Step>
### The moment it becomes paid

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   PBNK1/2026/00002         move_type=entry  state=posted  amount_total=163.4  payment_state=not_paid
      chg   INV/2026/00010           payment_state: partial -> paid
account.move.line   +2
      new   Manual Payment: INV/2026/00010 account_id=Outstanding Receipts  debit=163.4
      new   Manual Payment: INV/2026/00010 account_id=Account Receivable  credit=163.4  reconciled=True
      chg   S00025 - INV/2026/00010  reconciled: None -> True
account.payment     +1
      new   PBNK1/2026/00002         state=paid  amount=163.4  payment_type=inbound
      chg   PBNK1/2026/00001         state: in_process -> paid
```

The important line is the one that changed, not the ones that were created:

```text
chg   S00025 - INV/2026/00010  reconciled: None -> True
```

That is **the invoice's own receivable line** flipping to reconciled. It stayed unmatched
through the first payment and only flipped once the second one covered the rest. The
`payment_state: partial -> paid` on the line above it is the *consequence*.

</Step>

<Step>
### Look at the links themselves

```python
>>> inv.payment_state, inv.amount_residual   # settled
('paid', 0.0)
>>> recs = env["account.partial.reconcile"].search([("debit_move_id.move_id", "=", inv.id)])
>>> len(recs), recs.mapped("amount")
(2, [200.0, 163.4])
```

Two reconciliation records, one per instalment, summing to 363.40. Those two rows are
what "paid" actually means. Delete them and the invoice is unpaid again, whatever
`payment_state` said a moment earlier.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Verify

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py check ch28 --db functional
✔ a customer invoice for the brake pads was posted
✔ it balances and carries a tax line
✔ it is fully paid
✔ the receivable line is reconciled
```

The last two are deliberately separate. An invoice can read `paid` while something is
wrong underneath, so the check looks at the mechanism as well as the summary.

## Gotchas

- **`payment_state` is computed, so writing it fixes nothing.** It summarises
  `amount_residual` and the `reconciled` flags. If an invoice looks wrong, the repair is
  at the reconciliation level, and a script that writes `payment_state` directly produces
  books that lie.
- **`sale.order._create_invoices` is private and cannot be called over RPC.** Automating
  invoicing means driving the `sale.advance.payment.inv` wizard, which is also what the
  button does. The leading underscore is the contract.
- **Creating an invoice is not posting one.** The wizard leaves a draft, with `name` of
  `False` and zero accounting effect. "The invoice exists but the reports don't show it"
  is nearly always this.
- **A payment's own journal entry is separate from the invoice's.** They meet only
  through `account.partial.reconcile`. Two lines on the same receivable account are not
  connected until something reconciles them, which is why "the amounts look right but the
  invoice is unpaid" is possible.
- **Outstanding Receipts is not your bank balance.** A registered payment debits an
  outstanding/transit account first; matching it to the real bank statement is a second
  step. An `account.payment` sitting in `in_process` has not touched the bank account
  yet, so treating it as cash received overstates the balance.
