**Checkpoint:** none, this part continues in the `functional` demo database

## Why this matters

You do not need to be an accountant to work on Odoo. You do need to stop being surprised
by accounting, because a large share of real tickets touch it: an invoice with the wrong
tax, a report that does not tie, a payment that will not reconcile, a month that will not
close.

There is exactly one idea to internalise, and it is this chapter's title. **An invoice
and a journal entry are the same model.** Not similar, not related by a foreign key: the
same table, the same class, told apart by one field. Every developer who has not
internalised that eventually writes code that treats invoices as a special case and then
cannot explain why the accounting is wrong.

So this chapter does it in the honest order. First you hand-write a journal entry, which
forces you to meet debits and credits with nothing else in the way. Then you go and look
at the invoice chapter 22 produced, and find it is built from exactly the same parts.

## Concepts

### Double-entry, in the only two sentences you need

Every transaction is recorded at least twice: once as a **debit**, once as a **credit**,
and the two totals must be equal. That is the whole rule.

What debits and credits *mean* depends on the account, and the mnemonic that actually
survives contact with software is positional rather than moral:

- A **debit** increases an asset or an expense, and decreases a liability, equity or
  income.
- A **credit** does the reverse.

So buying a 250.00 tool cabinet with money from the bank is a debit of 250.00 to an
equipment account (an expense went up) and a credit of 250.00 to the bank account (an
asset went down). Nothing was created or destroyed; value moved, and the entry records
both ends. This is the same shape as chapter 25's stock moves, where goods always come
*from* one location and go *to* another. Accounting does it with money.

Odoo does not treat balance as a convention you should respect. It **refuses to post an
unbalanced entry**, which you will see for yourself.

### The three models

<Mermaid
  chart={`flowchart TD
    J["account.journal<br/>Sales, Purchases, Bank, Misc"]
    M["account.move<br/>one document<br/>(move_type says which kind)"]
    L1["account.move.line<br/>debit 250.00<br/>account: Purchase of Equipments"]
    L2["account.move.line<br/>credit 250.00<br/>account: Bank"]
    A["account.account<br/>the chart of accounts"]
    J --> M
    M --> L1
    M --> L2
    L1 -.-> A
    L2 -.-> A`}
/>

- **`account.account`** is the chart of accounts: the list of buckets value can sit in.
  Each carries an `account_type` (`asset_receivable`, `asset_cash`, `expense`, `income`,
  `equity`, and so on) which is what reports group by. The demo database installs a
  generic chart of 157 accounts.
- **`account.journal`** is the book an entry is written in, and it owns the numbering
  sequence. Sales, Purchases, Bank and Miscellaneous are the ones you will meet first.
- **`account.move`** is one accounting document, and **`account.move.line`** are its
  debit and credit lines. The lines are where the money actually is.

### The duality, stated plainly

`account.move` has a `move_type` field, and this is its entire selection:

| `move_type` | What it is |
|---|---|
| `entry` | Journal Entry (a plain, hand-written or machine-written entry) |
| `out_invoice` | Customer Invoice |
| `out_refund` | Customer Credit Note |
| `in_invoice` | Vendor Bill |
| `in_refund` | Vendor Credit Note |
| `out_receipt` | Sales Receipt |
| `in_receipt` | Purchase Receipt |

There is no `account.invoice` model. There has not been one for years. An invoice is an
`account.move` whose `move_type` is `out_invoice`, with the same balanced
`account.move.line` children as any other entry, plus some invoice-flavoured fields on
top. Its "invoice lines" and its "journal items" are the same records looked at through
two different views.

Once you see it this way, a lot of Odoo stops being surprising: why invoices balance, why
posting an invoice affects reports immediately, why you can find an invoice's accounting
impact without leaving the model you started on.

### Draft, posted, and where numbers come from

A move is `draft` until you post it. Two things follow, and both matter:

- **A draft move has no number.** Its `name` is `False`, not a placeholder. The journal's
  sequence is asked for a number at *posting* time.
- That is precisely why accounting numbering has no gaps: a number is never spent on
  something that might be abandoned. It also means numbering is legally defensible, which
  is the point of the whole arrangement.

Chapter 34's sequences are the same mechanism you already met, applied where the stakes
are highest.

## Hands-on

Two movements again, continuing in the `functional` database.

### Movement 1: run the flow

<Callout type="warn" title="Half the accounting menus are missing, on purpose">
  Community's accounting app is called **Invoicing** (the one called Accounting is the
  Enterprise one). More surprising: several menus you will look for are not in it. Chart of
  Accounts, Journal Entries and the accounting reports are gated behind a group called
  **Show Accounting Features - Readonly** (`account.group_account_readonly`), and Community
  grants it to nobody. The Accounting privilege on the user form offers only *Invoicing*
  and *Administrator*, neither of which implies it, and Odoo 19's privilege-based user form
  has no checkbox for it at all. In Enterprise, installing `account_accountant` grants it,
  which is why every screenshot online has these menus and you do not.

  The **actions are not gated, only the menus are**, so this chapter reaches those two
  screens by URL. Worth remembering as a technique: when a menu is missing but you know the
  action exists, `/odoo/action-<module>.<xmlid>` gets you there.
</Callout>

#### 1. Look at the chart of accounts and the journals

Open the chart of accounts at
`http://localhost:8069/odoo/action-account.action_account_form`, sort by code and scroll.
Note the **Type** column: that is `account_type`, and it is what makes a report able to
say "assets" without hard-coding account numbers.

Then **Invoicing → Configuration → Journals** (it sits under the *Accounting* heading in
that dropdown). Seven for this company:

```console
$ docker compose exec odoo odoo shell -c /etc/odoo/odoo.conf -d functional --no-http
```

```python
>>> for j in env["account.journal"].search([("company_id", "=", env.company.id)]):
...     print(j.code, j.name, j.type)
...
INV Sales sale
BILL Purchases purchase
BNK1 Bank bank
MISC Miscellaneous Operations general
CABA Cash Basis Taxes general
EXCH Exchange Difference general
STJ Inventory Valuation general
```

Two of those are worth noticing now. **STJ, Inventory Valuation**, is where chapter 30's
stock accounting lands. **CABA** and **EXCH** exist so tax-on-payment and currency
movements have somewhere to go, which is a good early hint that Odoo would rather create
a journal than fudge a number.

#### 2. Try to post an entry that does not balance

Journal entries are the other gated screen, so open them the same way:
`http://localhost:8069/odoo/action-account.action_move_journal_line`

Press **New**, pick the **Miscellaneous Operations** journal, and add two lines:

- Debit 250.00 to `611000 Purchase of Equipments`, labelled `Workshop tool cabinet`
- Credit **200.00** to `101401 Bank`, labelled `Paid from bank`

Press **Post**. Odoo stops you:

```text
The entry is not balanced.
```

That is not a warning you can dismiss. Double-entry is enforced at the database boundary,
not left to your good intentions.

#### 3. Balance it and post

Change the credit to **250.00** and post. The entry gets a number, and the status becomes
Posted.

### Movement 2: read what it did

#### 4. The number arrives on posting, not on creation

Take a snapshot before you post, and watch the two states from the shell:

```python
>>> mv.name, mv.state          # before posting
(False, 'draft')
>>> mv.name, mv.state          # after posting
('MISC/2026/08/0002', 'posted')
```

`False`, not `"/"` and not an empty string. There was no number to show, because none had
been requested. Your own number will differ, and it encodes the journal code and the
period: `MISC`, year, month, counter.

Then confirm the rule Odoo just enforced:

```python
>>> sum(mv.line_ids.mapped('debit')), sum(mv.line_ids.mapped('credit'))
(250.0, 250.0)
>>> mv.move_type
'entry'
```

#### 5. What the diff saw

```console
$ python3 odoolings.py diff --db functional
account.move        +1
      new   MISC/2026/08/0002        move_type=entry  state=posted  amount_total=250.0  payment_state=not_paid
account.move.line   +2
      new   Workshop tool cabinet    account_id=Purchase of Equipments  debit=250.0
      new   Paid from bank           account_id=Bank  credit=250.0
```

One document, two lines, one debit, one credit, equal amounts. That is a complete
accounting transaction and there is nothing else hiding behind it.

#### 6. Now go and look at an invoice

This is the payoff. Ask the model how many of each kind of document it is holding:

```python
>>> M = env["account.move"]
>>> for mt, label in M._fields["move_type"].selection:
...     n = M.search_count([("move_type", "=", mt)])
...     if n:
...         print(f"{mt:12} {n}")
...
entry        26
out_invoice  18
out_refund   8
in_invoice   4
in_refund    2
```

Five different document types, one model, one table. (If you reach for `read_group`
here instead, note that in Odoo 19 the count comes back under a key named after the
grouping field, `move_type_count`, not the `__count` older code expects.) Now open a real customer invoice and
check what it is made of:

```python
>>> inv = env["account.move"].search([("move_type", "=", "out_invoice"),
...                                   ("state", "=", "posted"),
...                                   ("company_id", "=", env.company.id)], limit=1)
>>> inv.name, inv.move_type, inv._name
('INV/2026/00008', 'out_invoice', 'account.move')
>>> for l in inv.line_ids:
...     print(l.account_id.code, l.account_id.name, l.debit, l.credit)
...
400000 Product Sales 0.0 750.0
121000 Account Receivable 750.0 0.0
```

Read those two lines like the entry you just wrote. The customer owes you 750.00, so
**Account Receivable is debited** (an asset went up). You earned 750.00, so **Product
Sales is credited** (income went up). Same structure, same balance, same model. The only
thing that made it look like "an invoice" was `move_type` and a different form view.

```python
>>> sum(inv.line_ids.mapped('debit')) == sum(inv.line_ids.mapped('credit'))
True
```

## Gotchas

- **A draft move's `name` is `False`, not `"/"` or `""`.** Code that formats
  `move.name` for a draft entry prints `False` into a report, and code that tests
  `if move.name` behaves differently before and after posting. If you need a label for an
  unposted move, use `display_name`.
- **`account.account.code` is computed, not stored, and company-dependent in Odoo 19.**
  Reading it while a different company is active returns `False`, silently. Verified: the
  same line read as the wrong company gives `False` and as the right one gives
  `'400000'`. Use `account.with_company(move.company_id).code`, and be very careful with
  psql queries against a `code` column, because the value lives in
  `account.code.mapping` rather than on the account row.
- **The shell does not apply the company filter the interface does.** This demo database
  has three companies and fourteen journals, but only seven belong to the active one. A
  bare `search([])` in a script crosses company boundaries in a way the same action in
  the interface would not, which is a genuinely dangerous difference when you are writing
  a fix against production data.
- **Do not look for `account.invoice`.** It does not exist. Searching core for it, or
  writing `env["account.invoice"]`, is the clearest sign of a tutorial or answer written
  for Odoo 8 or 9.
- **Unbalanced means unpostable, so "my code posted a broken entry" is never the bug.**
  If a move will not post, the arithmetic really is wrong, usually because a rounding
  difference or a tax line was computed rather than taken from Odoo.
