**Goal:** stop treating a tax as a percentage and start treating it as a percentage plus a
routing table, which is what Odoo actually stores. · **Time:** ~3 h ·
**Checkpoint:** none, this part continues in the `functional` demo database

## Why this matters

Chapter 28 posted a three-line invoice and pointed at the middle line: product revenue,
**tax**, receivable. Then it moved on. That single line is where a surprising share of
real accounting bugs live, because four different things can be wrong with it and each
one has a different field behind it:

- the tax landed on the wrong account, so revenue is overstated and nobody notices until
  the tax return does not tie out
- the tax was added on top of a price that already contained it, so every retail total is
  15% too high
- the tax was computed at the wrong scope, so a cent goes missing between the lines and
  the total
- the tax was not swapped for a foreign customer, so an export was charged domestic tax

None of those are exotic. All four are things you can cause with one wrong click, and all
four are invisible on the invoice PDF. This chapter is about being able to look at an
`account.tax` record and say what the journal entry will contain before you press
**Confirm**.

## Concepts

### A tax is a rate plus a routing table

The rate is the easy half. `account.tax` holds `amount` (15.0), `amount_type`
(`percent`, `fixed`, `division`, or `group`) and `type_tax_use` (`sale`, `purchase` or
`none`, which is what keeps purchase taxes out of the customer-invoice dropdown).

The half people skip is the children: a <Term k="repartition line (`account.tax.repartition.line`)">`account.tax.repartition.line`</Term>, labelled
**Distribution** in the interface. Each one answers "where does a piece of this go", and
each carries four things that matter:

| Field | Interface label | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `document_type` | (no column) | `invoice` or `refund`. The tax form never shows it, because it is implied by which of the two tables the line is in |
| `repartition_type` | **Based On** | `base` (the untaxed amount) or `tax` (shown as **of tax**) |
| `factor_percent` | **%** | how much of it this line takes |
| `account_id` | **Account** | where it lands |

A plain tax has four of them: base and tax, for invoices and for refunds. That is why
the shell prints four lines for a tax that looks like a single number in the interface.

The consequence worth holding on to: **one tax can produce several journal lines.** Not
one. Several. A US sales tax that splits between a state and a city, a tax with a
deductible and a non-deductible portion, a reverse charge that records both sides and
nets to nothing: all of them are one `account.tax` with more than one **of tax**
distribution line.

Odoo checks some of this for you, in an `@api.constrains` on `account.tax`: each table
needs exactly one base line, the two tables must have the same number of lines with the
same percentages in the same order, and the positive **of tax** factors must total 100%
(negative ones, if present, must total -100%). The tax form will refuse to save
otherwise. Write straight onto the child model from a script and you skip all of that,
which is the subject of this chapter's break-it lab.

### Included or excluded, and where that decision now lives

Two ways to charge 15%:

| | price you type | untaxed | tax | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tax **excluded** | 100.00 | 100.00 | 15.00 | 115.00 |
| tax **included** | 100.00 | 86.96 | 13.04 | 100.00 |

Same rate, same typed number, different money. Excluded adds on top. Included treats what
you typed as the total and works backwards: `100 / 1.15`, which is 86.9565…, and the
first thing you learn about tax-included pricing is that it almost never divides cleanly.

In Odoo 19, <Term k="price-included tax (`price_include`)">`price_include`</Term> is no longer the field you set.
`price_include` is **computed**, so writing it does nothing. What you set is
`price_include_override`, a selection of `tax_included` / `tax_excluded` shown as
**Included in Price** on the tax's Advanced Options tab, and if you leave it empty the
tax falls back to the company's `account_price_include`. Three fields, one answer:

```python
price_include = (override == 'tax_included'
                 or (company_price_include == 'tax_included' and not override))
```

The company-wide setting has a catch that matters the moment you touch a live database:
**it locks once any journal entry exists.** The interface marks it read-only, which is
the correct call (flipping it would silently reinterpret every price in the system) and
which means that on any database with history, the per-tax override is your only way in.

### Tax groups group, and nothing else

A <Term k="tax group (`account.tax.group`)">`account.tax.group`</Term> does not take part in the computation at all. It does two jobs: it
decides how taxes are aggregated in the totals block on an invoice (so that two 15% taxes
show as one "Tax 15%" subtotal rather than two lines), and it carries the
`tax_receivable_account_id` / `tax_payable_account_id` pair that the period-end tax
closing entry uses. If a total looks oddly grouped, look here. If a number is wrong, do
not.

### Fiscal positions, and the direction Odoo 19 reversed

A <Term k="fiscal position (`account.fiscal.position`)">fiscal position</Term> is the answer to "this customer is not taxed like the others". It maps
taxes (and optionally accounts) for a given customer or country, and Odoo resolves it
when a document is created.

If you have worked with fiscal positions before, the model changed under you. Through
Odoo 18 a fiscal position owned a one2many of `account.fiscal.position.tax` rows, each a
`tax_src_id` → `tax_dest_id` pair. In Odoo 19 that model is **gone**. The mapping now
lives on the tax, in the opposite direction: a replacement tax lists the domestic taxes
it stands in for.

<Mermaid
  label="Fiscal position tax mapping reversed direction in Odoo 19: the replacement tax now names the domestic tax it stands in for, instead of the position owning a source-to-destination mapping table"
  chart={`flowchart TB
    subgraph old["Odoo 18 and earlier"]
      FP1["fiscal position<br/>Foreign Trade"] -- "tax_ids: one2many" --> M["account.fiscal.position.tax<br/>src 15% / dest 0% Exports"]
    end
    subgraph new["Odoo 19"]
      FP2["fiscal position<br/>Foreign Trade"] -- "tax_ids: many2many" --> TX["account.tax<br/>0% Exports"]
      TX -- "original_tax_ids<br/>(Replaces)" --> DOM["account.tax<br/>15%"]
    end`}
/>

So there are three fields to read instead of one table:

- **`account.tax.fiscal_position_ids`**, on the tax, labelled **Fiscal Position**
  (singular, though it accepts several) with the placeholder *all*: which positions this
  tax belongs to. Empty means it applies everywhere.
- **`account.tax.original_tax_ids`**, labelled **Replaces**: which domestic taxes this
  one stands in for.
- **`account.tax.is_domestic`**, stored and computed: true when the tax has no fiscal
  positions, or belongs to the company's own `domestic_fiscal_position_id`.

`account.fiscal.position.tax_map` is then computed by walking that backwards, and
`map_tax()` applies it. Note the map's values are **lists**: one domestic tax can be
replaced by more than one tax at once.

Resolution order, when a document is created, is worth knowing precisely because "why did
it pick that one" is a recurring question. A fiscal position set by hand on the contact
wins outright. Otherwise Odoo takes every position with **Detect Automatically** on,
sorts company-specific ones first and then by `sequence`, and returns the first one whose
criteria all pass: VAT-required, zip range, federal state, country, country group. And
before any of that, there is a short circuit worth remembering: **a contact with no
country gets no fiscal position at all.**

### Rounding: per line, or per tax

Fifteen percent of 10.10 is 1.515. There is no such coin. Somebody has to round, and the
only question is whether they round once per line or once for the whole tax:

| three lines of 10.10, 15% | tax | total |
|---|---|---|
| **Round per Line**: 1.515 → 1.52, three times | 4.56 | 34.86 |
| **Round per Tax**: 30.30 × 15% = 4.545, once | 4.55 | 34.85 |

One cent, and neither answer is wrong. They serve different masters: rounding per line
keeps the line totals adding up to the document total, which is what you want when prices
are tax-included and a customer can check your arithmetic on the shelf label. Rounding
per tax keeps the tax exactly proportional to the base, which is what a tax authority
wants.

<Term k="tax rounding (`tax_calculation_rounding_method`)">`res.company.tax_calculation_rounding_method`</Term> holds it, and two details about that field
catch people out. Its stored values are `round_per_line` and `round_globally`, but the
Odoo 19 interface labels the second one **Round per Tax**, so grepping the interface for
"globally" finds nothing. And the Odoo 19 default is `round_globally`, where Odoo 18's
was `round_per_line`.

<Callout type="warn" title="On Odoo 18 this differs">
  Two of this chapter's mechanisms are Odoo 19 changes. Fiscal positions in 18 and
  earlier map taxes through `account.fiscal.position.tax` rows on the position, not
  through `original_tax_ids` on the tax. And the default rounding method in 18 is
  `round_per_line`, labelled "Round Globally" for the other option. Existing databases
  keep whatever value they were on, so an upgraded 18 database and a fresh 19 one can
  disagree by a cent on the same order.
</Callout>

### Where an unaccounted tax actually goes

The tax-included invoice from step 2:


Nothing was misconfigured on purpose. A brand new tax's distribution lines have no
account, and Odoo's fallback for a tax line with no account is the account the base line
used. So the tax quietly becomes revenue, the receivable is still right, the customer
still owes 215.00, and the only symptom is that revenue is overstated by exactly the tax
you owe onward. This is a large fraction of the "why is the P&L wrong" tickets in the
world, and it is one empty cell.

and `100.00` typed produced `86.96`. Tax-included prices are the only case where two
lines of different revenue can add up to a round total.

Two of those are doing more than they look. The second one checks that each tax line's
share of the total matches **its own** distribution factor, not just that two lines
exist, because two lines splitting 50/50 when the table says 60/40 would be a real bug
and an easy one to miss. And the export check requires the line's tax to declare an
`original_tax_ids`, because zero tax on its own would also be true of a line with no tax
at all, which is a different and worse situation.

## Gotchas

- **A new tax has no account on its distribution lines, and Odoo will not warn you.**
  The fallback is the account the base line used, so the tax lands in income and the
  invoice still balances. Step 2 of the hands-on does this on purpose so you can see how
  invisible it is. Always check both the invoice and the refund table.
- **`price_include` is computed in Odoo 19, so writing it does nothing.** Set
  `price_include_override`. The company-wide default that the override falls back to is
  read-only once the database has a single journal entry, so on any real database the
  per-tax override is the only lever you have.
- **`compute_all` is public but unusable over RPC.** It is the right tool from
  `odoo shell` and it is unreachable from an XML-RPC client, because its return dict
  carries a recordset under `group` and the marshaller gives up:
  `KeyError: <class 'odoo.orm.models.account.tax'>`. If you need tax amounts from
  outside, build a draft `account.move` and read its lines.
- **A fiscal position with no taxes listed is not a no-op in Odoo 19.** `map_tax()` falls
  back to dropping every tax that belongs to *any* fiscal position, so an empty position
  applied to a customer produces untaxed invoices rather than normally taxed ones. In
  Odoo 18 an empty mapping left the taxes alone. Related: a contact with no country gets
  no fiscal position at all, and setting the country afterwards does not fix quotations
  that already exist.
- **Totals are stored, and a manual tax beats the mapping.** Changing the rounding method
  or a fiscal position does not recompute documents that were already computed, so an
  order confirmed last week keeps last week's cent. And a tax typed into the line by hand
  survives the fiscal position, which is exactly what you want when a consultant
  overrides one order and exactly what confuses you when they did it three months ago.

  alive on top of the new one.

  `_get_fpos_validation_functions` is the auto-apply criteria as executable code, and
